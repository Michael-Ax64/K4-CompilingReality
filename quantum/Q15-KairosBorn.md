## III. $T$-Symmetric Conjugate Channels

The reaction pairs connecting adjacent heavy nuclei — $^{197}\text{Au}(\alpha, p)^{200}\text{Hg}$ and its reverse — are **$T$-symmetric conjugate channels**, not self-fueling engines:

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Forward: } & {}^{197}_{79}\text{Au} + {}^{4}_{2}\alpha \longrightarrow {}^{200}_{80}\text{Hg} + {}^{1}_{1}\text{p} \quad (Q = -6.50\text{ MeV}) \\
\text{Reverse: } & {}^{200}_{80}\text{Hg} + {}^{1}_{1}\text{p} \longrightarrow {}^{197}_{79}\text{Au} + {}^{4}_{2}\alpha \quad (Q = +6.50\text{ MeV})
\end{aligned}$$

1. **Energy conservation.** The forward reaction is endothermic, $Q = -6.50$ MeV, with a laboratory threshold of $6.63$ MeV and a Coulomb barrier of roughly $22$–$26$ MeV for the $\alpha$ on gold. It cannot run spontaneously ($P_{\text{diss}} > 0$).
2. **Asymmetric cross-sections.** $\sigma(\alpha, p) \neq \sigma(p, \alpha)$ at identical centre-of-mass energies.
3. **Gamma bleeding.** The compound nucleus $^{201}$Tl$^*$ sheds energy through prompt $\gamma$ emission.
