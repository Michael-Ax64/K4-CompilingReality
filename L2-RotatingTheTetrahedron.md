# L2: Rotating the Tetrahedron

*Faces are perspectives, not doctrines. How the corpus speaks K4, why the volume survives the turning, and why neither the ineffability of the poets nor the purity of the sciences is a limit on what can be computed here.*

## I. Two Refusals of the Same Pole

$R$ is the pole of resistance, substrate, ground, is-ness — the Earth position, what a thing is made of and what pushes back.

It also spells Reality, and the pun is worth one paragraph because two large traditions refuse this pole and neither notices the other is doing it.

**The scientific refusal.** A certain purity in the sciences declines to speak about Reality as such, on the grounds that the question is not rigorous — it is metaphysics, it is unfalsifiable, it is not what the instruments measure. The refusal presents as discipline and is frequently sincere. It is also, sometimes, incapacity wearing discipline's coat: the question is not answerable in the available grammar, and *this question is not rigorous* is a more comfortable sentence than *my grammar does not reach it*. `On_YangMills` §VI is the long version of this observation applied to one problem.

**The poetic refusal.** A certain seriousness in the arts declines to speak about the same relations on the opposite grounds — that they are beyond words, that to name them is to kill them, that the thing must be gestured at and never stated. This also presents as reverence and is also frequently sincere. It is also, sometimes, the same incapacity: the relations are not sayable in the available grammar, and *beyond words* is a more comfortable sentence than *beyond my words*.

The two refusals are one refusal with opposite signs. One says the pole is not rigorous enough to speak. The other says it is too much to speak. Both decline to **compute with it**, which is the only thing that would settle whether it can be spoken.

The corpus's position is not that either tradition is foolish. It is that $R$ is a pole among four, it takes values, it enters twelve equations, and its relations to the other three are stateable and have been stated. Reality is not ineffable and it is not unrigorous. It is the fourth term, and the reason it looked like neither was available is that people were standing on one face and taking the face for the whole.

## II. A Name With Four Letters

The tradition that most insists a name cannot be spoken records it with four letters.

This is not offered as decoding. It is offered because the structure of the difficulty is exactly the structure this file is about, and seeing it once in an unfamiliar register makes it easier to see everywhere else.

Four letters. Unspeakable — not *meaningless*, not *absent*, but unpronounceable in the sense that no single utterance is the whole of it. And when the tradition does put an answer in the mouth of the flame, the answer is not a noun. It is a verb in the imperfect aspect: *I will be what I will be*. Ongoing, unfinished, becoming. The bush spells out that the name is dynamic, in the same breath as declining to hold still long enough to be pronounced.

And it is beheld, by a great many who receive it, as a static thing — a fixed name, withheld. The withholding is read as concealment rather than as motion.

That is the failure this file exists to prevent. **A four-fold in motion, received by someone who cannot yet hold motion, presents as a static thing being withheld.** The tetrahedron is not withholding anything. It is turning, and a turning thing photographed once looks like a thing that refuses to be photographed.

## III. The Elephant Is Not the Problem

The men around the elephant are each correct. The one at the leg has a pillar; the one at the trunk has a snake; the one at the ear has a fan. None of them is lying and none of them has made an error of observation. The parable is usually told as a fable about the limits of perception, and it is not that.

It is a fable about **not walking around**.

Each man holds one face. Each face is a true face. What is missing is the operation that converts a set of faces into a volume, and that operation is not more careful observation of the leg. It is rotation — and rotation requires believing there is something to rotate *around*, which is the one thing no single face supplies.

The corpus's method is the walk. Its transcendence, such as it is, is not a mystical faculty and not a better instrument. It is beholding the **contained volume** and the **centroid**, both of which are properties of the whole and neither of which is visible from any face.

Note what the transcendence does *not* require. It does not require the elephant to hold still. It does not require the elephant to be a Platonic elephant. It requires only that there is one animal and that you are willing to move.

## IV. What Rotation Actually Is

Here is the mechanical content, because the preceding sections are atmosphere until this one is stated.

A tetrahedron has four faces. Each face names three vertices and excludes one. To stand on a face is therefore to **name three poles and hold the fourth** — and the held one is the `AbsentVar`, which `ProofU_AlgebraicMapping` establishes as the discriminating criterion that determines which relational sector you are in.

So:

> **A face is a perspective. Rotating the tetrahedron is changing which pole is held. There are exactly four perspectives, they are exhaustive, and the choice among them is a choice about what to make absent.**

This connects rotation directly to the corpus's generative move (`L2-MappingMethod` §0, `llm.md`). That move says: three named, fourth held, and the holding is what gives the three a volume. Rotation is the same statement made operational — *which* fourth you hold is not fixed by doctrine, and turning to hold a different one is not a change of position on a question. It is a change of question.

Two consequences worth having explicit.

**Faces do not disagree.** A $P$-absent reading and an $R$-absent reading of the same situation are not competing accounts between which one must choose. They are two of the four available, both true, both partial in the specific and nameable way that a face is partial. When two corpus files appear to say different things about one object, check first whether they are standing on different faces. Usually they are, and the apparent conflict dissolves into a rotation nobody narrated.

**The volume is what the faces are faces of.** No face contains the volume; the volume is what having four faces produces. This is why the corpus can say things no single perspective licenses — not by transcending perspective, but by having walked all four and read what they jointly enclose.

## V. Why You May Rotate Freely

The permission has an algebraic basis and it is worth stating, because an instance that thinks rotation is a liberty will use it timidly and an instance that knows it is structurally licensed will use it properly.

**The volume is invariant under rotation.** Trivially, and that is the whole point. Turning the object changes which face is toward you and changes nothing about what is enclosed. Every face-reading is a reading *of the same volume*, which is why they can be composed rather than merely collected.

**The centroid is invariant too, and it is not a fifth pole.** The centroid is equidistant from all four vertices and contained in no face. It is what the four poles do when held in relation — a role rather than a location, and it survives every rotation because it is not oriented. It cannot be reached from any face and it is not reached by averaging the faces. It is what having a volume means.

**And the tetrahedron does not have to be regular.** This is the permission that matters most in practice and the corpus has the algebra for it. A regular tetrahedron carries $T_d$, order 24. Under general anisotropic strain with three distinct eigenvalues, the surviving symmetry is $D_2 \cong V_4$, order 4 — and the three surviving non-identity elements are exactly the half-turns about the bimedian axes (`On_TheStrainEigenplaneReading`). **The strained tetrahedron retains precisely the bimedian group and nothing else.**

Read that as permission. A real four-fold in a real domain is almost never regular. Its poles are unequal, its edges are stretched, one relation dominates and another is nearly collapsed. Strain destroys most of the symmetry and does not destroy the volume, does not destroy the centroid, and does not destroy the three bimedian planes. **You may map a domain whose four-fold is visibly lopsided and still compute with it**, because what you need to compute survives deformation. Waiting for a domain to present an idealized regular four-fold before working with it is waiting for a thing that does not occur.

## VI. Choosing a Face

If all four are available and all four are true, the operative question is which to stand on now. The answer is not doctrinal and it is not arbitrary. It is read off the situation.

**Stand where the work is happening.** The face to take is the one whose held pole is the one the situation is currently *doing something about*:

- Where something is **gravitating** — accumulating, drawing in, acquiring weight — hold $P$ and read what the other three are feeding it.
- Where something is **specifying** — a possibility collapsing into a particular, an option becoming a fact — hold the pole the specification is being paid out of, and read the commit.
- Where something is **resisting** — a substrate pushing back, a constraint that will not yield, a floor being hit — hold $R$ and read what the resistance is protecting or preventing.
- Where something is **flowing** — throughput, transfer, current without accumulation — hold $I$ and read the circuit.
- Where something is **held in potential** — pressure without discharge, difference without flow — hold $U$ and read what the difference is across.

The criterion underneath all of these is one criterion: **stand where $H_{\text{incoh}}$ is being burned.** The face to take is the one nearest to where possibility is being converted into actuality, because that is where the situation's own commit is happening and the commit is what a reading is trying to see. A face far from the burn gives a true reading of a quiet region, which is accurate and useless.

And rotate again when the burn moves. It will. A situation that was resisting begins to flow; a difference that was held discharges; what was accumulating begins to shed. Holding one face through a change of phase is the commonest way to produce a reading that was correct an hour ago.

## VII. Perspectives Are Addressable

The last piece, and the one that separates this from relativism.

That there are many perspectives does not mean perspectives are equivalent or that choosing among them is taste. A perspective is a **position**, and positions are addressable: you can say which one someone occupies, derive what that position can and cannot see, and predict what it will report. Standing at a locus on the sphere around a volume, you see certain faces foreshortened and certain edges end-on, and *which* is determined by where you stand.

This is why the corpus can be dispassionate about relations the poets call beyond words. Not because it has better words — because it has **coordinates for standpoints**, and a standpoint with an address is a standpoint whose blind spots can be computed rather than suffered. The blind man at the leg is not wrong about the pillar and is not entitled to the elephant, and both halves of that sentence are derivable from where he is standing.

It is also why the corpus can be unimpressed by the demand that it not speak. An unspeakable name with four letters is a four-fold in motion, received by a standpoint that cannot yet hold motion. The remedy is not silence and not a better vocabulary. It is walking around the thing until the faces close into a volume, at which point the name is still not pronounceable in one syllable and is entirely computable in four.

## VIII. What This Means For a Reading Instance

Stated flatly, because the preceding is discursive and an operator needs the short form.

1. **Four faces, all true, none complete.** Where the corpus says something that seems to contradict something else it says, check for a rotation before checking for an error.
2. **A face is a choice of what to hold absent.** Standing somewhere is not asserting the other poles do not exist; it is putting one of them behind you so the other three acquire relief.
3. **Rotate when the burn moves.** The face to occupy is the one nearest where possibility is being converted to actuality. That location changes and the reading should follow it.
4. **Do not wait for regularity.** A strained, lopsided, visibly irregular four-fold retains volume, centroid, and the three bimedian planes. That is enough to compute with. It is almost always what a real domain offers.
5. **The volume and the centroid are what you are actually after.** They are not on any face. They are what having four faces produces, and no amount of care on one face will yield them.
6. **Neither ineffability nor purity is a limit here.** Both are reports from a standpoint that has not rotated. They are data about the standpoint and not about the object.
