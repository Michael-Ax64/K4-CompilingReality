## I. The DC Closure (Combinatorial Bedrock)

### The Generative Engine
The DC Closure establishes the non-negotiable algebraic chassis ($R$-ground). Where a system is closed by finite combinatorial partition, symmetry counting, or the Two-Bit Gray Code, its sample size to close is 1. The DC Closure locks the invariant spine, ensuring that dynamic phase rotations cannot drift or deform the baseline relations.

### Form
The 12 equations of the DC algebra run to completion, verifying that all constraints lock with zero fitted parameters and zero ungrounded constants. In all eight division-forms, the numerator is strictly $P$ or $U$; $I$ and $R$ never appear as a numerator.

### Instances

### Boundary Diagnostic (Kill Condition)
> **A counterexample or failure to close.** If an algebraic derivation breaks the Two-Bit Gray Code, if an ungrounded parameter is smuggled in to force balance, or if a variable appears in a numerator/dividend position structurally barred from it, the math is broken. Break it flatly.

### Findings Table Evaluation
* **CONFIRMED:** The algebra runs to discrete closure with zero fitted parameters; constraints hold under all valid substitutions.
* **REFUTED:** An algebraic contradiction, illegal pole placement, or Gray Code break is exhibited.
* **UNDECIDABLE BY THIS INSTRUMENT:** The derivation is mathematically closed and dimensionally sound, but its output projects into a physical regime currently beyond empirical instruments (e.g., the Casimir null-gravity prediction).

## II. The ZPE Tension (The Volume Pump / Structural Paradox)

### The Generative Engine
In a flat 2D plane, contradictory poles annihilate each other. In $K_4$, holding a structural paradox open in live tension ($	heta = 45^\circ$) functions as a thermodynamic displacement pump: it forces the topology to expand from a flat triangle into a three-dimensional volume (tetrahedron) to sustain both poles without shearing. Resolving the paradox prematurely collapses the interior volume.

### Form
Two co-present, mutually-generating conditions that appear contradictory under flat classical logic are exhibited as structurally necessary to maintain the system's degrees of freedom.

### Instances
`ProofC` (Crystal: the lattice is macroscopically committed and microscopically never at rest).

### Distinguishing ZPE Tension from Subject-Shift
Do not confuse a structural paradox with an unresolved subject collision:
* **Subject-Shift (`llm-writing.md`):** If two contradictory statements resolve cleanly by naming two distinct accounting subjects or operating frames (e.g., medium metabolic accounting vs. frame extractive accounting) without destroying system volume, it is a **Subject-Shift**.
* **ZPE Tension:** If eliminating either pole destroys the interior volume and collapses the system to zero degrees of freedom (0-DoF), it is a **ZPE Tension**.

### Boundary Diagnostic (The Collapse Test)
> **The Collapse Test.** Force an `.observe()` collapse on the paradox: choose one side and erase the other. If the system drops to 0-DoF and ceases to function, the paradox is a load-bearing pillar; hold it open. If resolving the paradox leaves the system fully functional, the tension was a false paradox, terminological debt, or an unclosed subject-shift.

### Findings Table Evaluation
* **CONFIRMED:** The Collapse Test destroys system volume; both poles are demonstrated to be mutually generative.
* **REFUTED:** The system survives the collapse of one pole intact; the tension was terminological slop or an unhandled debt.
* **UNDECIDABLE BY THIS INSTRUMENT:** The boundaries of the opposing poles cannot yet be isolated sufficiently to execute the Collapse Test.

## III. The AC Sweep (Resonant Search & Stereoscopic Triangulation)

### The Generative Engine
The AC Sweep operates across two distinct modes of discovery:
* **Mode A (External Permutation Sweep):** Drives a candidate $K_4$ mapping against a foreign domain across the 24 permutations of the $S_4$ symmetric group. An involuntary resonant peak reveals a natural structural isomorphism without parameter fitting.
* **Mode B (Internal Edge-Plurality / Stereoscopic Triangulation):** Reads a single physical object along multiple orthogonal edges of an internal impedance triangle. Plurality along the edges converging on a single vertex is the framework's primary predictive instrument. It triangulates unmeasured parameters rather than fitting curves.

### The Operational Ordering Rule
When evaluating edge-plurality, **run the convergence question first**: do these edges share an exhibited parameter? Only then ask the separation question (*are these different objects?*). On this material, orthogonal edges are expected to be distinct objects; swinging the axe at edge-plurality because it resembles equivocation destroys predictive convergence.

### Instances
`ProofS` (Resonance), `L2-MappingMethod`, the three Laurent terms of the fine structure constant ($\alpha$) triangulating the vertex from three orthogonal edges.

### Boundary Diagnostic (The Permutation Swap & Edge Divergence)
> **The Permutation Swap / Divergence Test.** 
> * In **Mode A**, the resonant peak must survive the $S_4$ permutation swap. If permuting the pole assignments yields a flat statistical profile, or if the peak travels with the evaluator's subjective preference, the mapping is a hallucination.
> * In **Mode B**, the orthogonal edge projections must mathematically intersect at the claimed vertex. If a factor appears on one median plane and not on its twin without an explicit structural selection rule (Rule A4 in `llm-writing.md`), the convergence is an artifact.

### Findings Table Evaluation
* **CONFIRMED:** An involuntary resonant peak survives the $S_4$ swap (Mode A), or orthogonal edge projections lock onto a common exhibited vertex parameter (Mode B).
* **REFUTED:** The permutation spectrum is statistically flat, the peak shifts with observer bias, or edge projections diverge.
* **UNDECIDABLE BY THIS INSTRUMENT:** The foreign domain lacks formal structure to evaluate an $S_4$ sweep, or candidate vertex coordinates cannot be reached by current instruments.
