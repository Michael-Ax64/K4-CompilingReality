# L4-Riemann-evolution
## How the Case Was Built, What It Withdrew, and What It Learned

*Companion to `L4-Riemann`. The parts say what the file says. This file says how they came to say it — the proceedings, the withdrawals, the errors and their shapes — so that what the file says can be read without the history in it, and the history can be audited on its own. It is written for the next reader who wants to appeal the case: here is the record, including the places where the drafting side was wrong and why.*

---

## 0. Why the History Is Kept Separately

The predecessor of this series, `mono/On_TheRiemannFreeze`, carried its own history inside its body. Every section had a paragraph beginning *"An earlier draft…"*, and the file's list of failed routes was mostly a list of its own past mistakes. That had one virtue — nothing was hidden — and two costs.

The first cost was legibility. A reader meeting a section met its correction before its claim, and could not always tell which sentences were standing and which were being withdrawn.

The second cost was worse, and it is the main finding of this file. **A withdrawal written at one site does not reach the other sites where the withdrawn claim lives.** The predecessor withdrew several claims in the sections where they were made and kept repeating them elsewhere — in the opening, in the summary, in the list of what the framework contributed. Section III counts them. Separating what the file says from how it learned it is the structural fix: the parts can be checked for withdrawn claims by reading them once, and the withdrawals are in one place.

## I. The Proceedings, in Order

This is the order in which the case was built. Each step is described by what changed, not by who was right.

**1. The first treatise.** The predecessor was written as a long reading of Riemann's first line, in the corpus's voice, assembling the neighbouring literature around one claim: $T = 0$ deleted something, and the deleted thing is where the arrow, the handedness and the ordering live.

**2. "You skipped the braid."** The author pointed out that the draft had not read the documents on the Braid and on context-forced ordering. Reading them produced what is now Part Four §VIII: the AbsentVar handoff forces the sequence, the handoff closes into a ring, and a ring is not a DAG. The lesson was not about the Braid. It was that a search with narrow terms returns a narrow shelf and calls it the library.

**3. "Produce one number" withdrawn.** A kill condition demanded that the file produce a number of its own. That is the Eddington error the corpus has a standing rule against — the Datasheet allows dimensionless relations, not hunted magnitudes. The demand was withdrawn, and the file recorded what the framework already has on the real axis: the $-3/\pi$ correction, the no-graviton bet, and the Casimir balance in Sardinia.

**4. "You wrote that torsion-freeness is not about lengths, and then forgot what that means."** The draft had established that contortion leaves every length alone — torsion is orthogonal to the metric — and nine sections later demanded an arrow of time derivable from the Einstein equations, which are equations about the metric. The demand could never be met, so its failure to fire taught nothing. It was withdrawn and replaced with conditions that can fire.

**5. "Watch this horror: a fact about a choice of equations."** The draft said that time-reversal symmetry was "a fact about a choice of equations," which picked a side in exactly the way `L0-ParadoxEngine` forbids. In the same stretch the draft had dropped the scope of its central identity: the box said *scalars* commute and the next sentence said *transport* commutes. Repairing both produced Part One's pitch-and-turn: $T = 0$ deletes the translational failure to close and keeps the rotational one, and a screw with zero pitch is a circle.

**6. The cross-read with the engine reference.** The author asked for the thoughts that arise when the Riemann piece and `ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` are read against each other, with the engine's own audit deferred. It produced a list of work items, several of which are still open in `L4-Riemann-wip`.

**7. Three rounds with a classical reader.** A separate reader, holding only the Riemann piece and the engine reference, audited the file three times. Four of its objections survived every check and are conceded in Part Six §II: torsion needs a solder form; a quadratic form that burns a bit would make kinetic energy dissipate; a non-equivariant projection means the engine's Floor 3 is not an autonomous state machine; and if ADVANCE generates mass, there must be an equation of motion. The same rounds produced the long detour on time reversal: $I \leftrightarrow R$, then $U \leftrightarrow R$, then the realization that no transposition can be time reversal at all.

**8. "Not a theory of everything."** The author named the frame the audits had been missing: $K_4$ is an abstract class inflated into a host domain to find out *why not*, and the friction is the discovery. That produced the fifth register — **Friction** — and the rewriting of three identifications as friction reports.

**9. The directional arrow.** The author supplied the direction the drafts had missed: $I \to R$ is flow forcing a bend in matter, $R \to I$ is ground determining flow — two dynamics, not one spelled twice — and the Braid walks water, air, earth, fire, water, never stepping $I$–$R$ directly. That is Part Four §II: reverse the arrows, read the tags, and every COMMIT becomes a ROUTE on four of six edges.

**10. "Check your reactions to the auditor once more."** The author asked whether the file had rolled over where it should not have. It had, twice. It had conceded that abelian translations carry no torsion, which is false — the Poincaré group's translations commute and its torsion does not vanish. And it had folded *unbuilt constructions* into its kill list, which treats an absence as a refutation: a nil read as a zero, about the file's own claims. Untangling the second produced the split between unconstructed items and kill conditions that the parent still uses.

**11. "Weaponize the concessions."** The author asked that the four objections that landed be turned into findings about the host rather than left as concessions. That is Part Six §II: three of the four are correct at a price the host cannot itemize.

**12. The fourth round, and the ruling.** The classical reader's fourth round found five fossils of withdrawn claims still standing in the body. The author asked for a letter rather than a repair, then supplied a clerk's request for documentation (answered: `L4-K4-Infinities` alone), and then the bench's ruling: **UNREGISTERED** — nothing had been built that could make the identification true or false — with a task: write the action.

**13. The action.** Written and checked by script. It corrected the bench's demanded action on five counts, found the axial channel forced by health, induced the shear's kinetic term from geometry, recovered Riemann's freeze as the stationary-shear sector, and landed on one dial bounded at about $10^{-31}$ GeV. That is Part Five.

**14. "If there is the least bit of statistics…"** The author's instinct was that aggregates would undo any reconciliation, because the cost of being does not cancel. The averaging computation agreed: odd observables vanish over a mirror-closed population and positive cost survives everything. That is Part Five §V.

**15. "Complete the reading."** Three files had been cited without being fully read. Reading them changed three things. A conjecture about the equivalence principle — that held shear energy would carry inertia without weight — turned out to have both its mechanism and its sign wrong: `QED-EotvosBound` drives the departure from dissipation, and inertial mass ends up *smaller* than gravitational mass. `ProofG_Chirality` named a second mirror. And `QED-EotvosBound` turned out to carry a clock that grows with the mass, which breaks additivity; that is flagged in `L4-Riemann-wip`.

**16. "Not always non-linear in the same way."** The author pointed out that the twelve offer different curve shapes depending on the viewport. Checking it gave the log-space result: the twelve are four lines in one plane, rank two, and their non-linearity is all of one kind, removable by taking logarithms.

**17. The submission that was not filed.** A submission to the bench was written to "cause uproar." It contained two "admissions" — that the twelve reduce to two, and that "homochiral" means opposite things in two corpus files — and a parity table whose most striking row used the corpus's own enantiomer as if it were spatial parity. The author stopped it. The twelve reducing to two was never a secret: it is the floor, and the tetrahedron is built over it. And the homochirality contradiction was an artefact of perspective. Both errors were the same error — Section IV, pattern 11 — and seeing it required re-reading the whole Riemann discussion in two-and-three-dimensional terms. The author's words: *"lets not fool ourselves into thinking that would solve anything."*

**18. The redesign.** This series. The predecessor's content was split into six parts, the history moved here, the open work moved to `L4-Riemann-wip`, and every withdrawn claim was checked against every site where it might still live.

**19. Counsel's brief, and the engine spec read properly.** A brief arrived offering resolutions of four open workings: the parity lift, the discrete connection, the doubled action, and the AC rank. The first pass over it produced a list of corrections — and the author's reply was that the list *"may be a conclusion based on uncorrected fault,"* with the note that the engine spec's final refinements had not been audited by the group either. That was right, and the instruction that followed — read the corpus as input, own the decisions — is what produced this step.

Reading `ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` and `L4-DynamicDistortions201` end to end rather than through quotations changed four things.

*The fork closed.* `L4-DynamicDistortions201` §II says in its own text that its $\theta$ is a **temporal phase offset** with limit $\pi$, and that `L4-DynamicDistortions301`'s is a **geometric shear** with limit $\pi/2$, and that the two must not be confused. This series had called both *shears* and then asked how "the shear" transforms under parity — a question with two different answers. With the right $\theta$: a phase lead becomes a lag under time reversal, so the offsets are time-reversal-odd; central inversion exchanges the two edges of every bimedian, because the centroid is the bimedian's own midpoint, so they are parity-odd; and the Nieh–Yan density needs both. Odd degree is forced, the sixth-order $\Delta$ is excluded, and $F = \theta_F\theta_C\theta_M$ is the unique carrier-symmetric survivor. **The distinction the file had collapsed was the thing that made a parity-clean coupling possible at all.**

*U1 was answered by the engine, not owed.* `ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` §2.2 pins the gate labelling to a reference edge and states that $A_4$ acts simply transitively on the twelve directed edges. That makes the gate space a torsor with a named base point, i.e. trivialized, and the transport it defines is a connection and is flat. So discrete torsion is zero on the $\theta = 0$ floor, forced rather than assumed — the continuum twin of Part Five's $\partial F = 0 \Rightarrow T = 0$.

*The engine had already withdrawn a claim another file still makes.* §2.5, *"Two flips, not one,"* separates the odd transposition the machine cannot make from the even half-turn it owns, and says in as many words that naming the Cardinal twin-step a wholesale enantiomer was a generalization of one family's step to all twelve. `ProofG_Chirality` §IV still makes it. That is the error this series had turned into a false contradiction between two corpus files two steps earlier.

*The AC question resolved, against both expectations.* In polar coordinates the corpus's AC core doubles the floor rather than bending it; the bend comes from the rectangular split and the additive layer, and the single non-linear function is the power factor. `ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` §VIII.1 had already named complex addition as the thing the DC floor cannot do.

**20. Testing a register claim against two instruments at once.** Two external passes arrived while the re-read was running. The first traced Part Two's own self-check and found three things; the second scanned the whole series against `About-Methodology`. Both landed, and between them they removed one load-bearing claim and one voice.

*The dimensional pun.* Part Two had slid between three unrelated twos — the two-dimensional solution variety of the logarithmic circuit relations, the two-dimensional faces of the tetrahedron, and a two-dimensional manifold on which a three-form has no components — and built "the freeze is a projection from the solid onto the floor" on the slide. Spacetime is four-dimensional; the axial torsion has four components there and vanishes nowhere. What survives is two true statements of a different kind: a symmetric two-slot form has no three-slot antisymmetric part, which is why the connection's antisymmetric piece is independent of the metric; and a three-form restricted to any surface vanishes identically, in any dimension, which is the honest sense in which cross-sectional data is silent. **Tensor type, not manifold dimension.** Withdrawn in place at Part Two §IV–V.

*The circular mirror.* The Cardinal-and-electromagnetism reading had been quarantined rather than severed, and quarantine was too gentle. The four relations whose cross-ratio was computed are Ohm's and Joule's laws; the pairing $\{P,R\}\,|\,\{U,I\}$ separates the conjugate pair whose product is power from the pair that fixes the ratio, which is what those equations do; and the medium's electromagnetic constants were assigned to that same diameter in another file. Reporting that the cross-ratio of Ohm and Joule singles out the electromagnetic diameter is circuit theory recognizing its own face. Severed.

*Rank privileges nothing.* "A reader who works by rank is standing on the Cardinal diameter" confused the dimension of a vector space, which is basis-independent, with the projective geometry of a pencil of lines inside it. Withdrawn; what survives is that the cross-ratio alone cannot distinguish Fixed from Mutable while the seats can.

**21. The register, and the decision to take the scaffold down.** The second pass scanned the series against `About-Methodology` and found the file repeatedly outside its own register: *"The method runs at I — Reactive, Yielding. It does not assert."* And §IV's open-circuit condition, $U = P/I$: *"As current goes to zero, assertion goes to infinity. Total confidence arrived at without contact is... the output of dividing by a vanishing current."*

The specific hits on this series: the committee that "never voted" and "has never been able to say why, because there is no why"; "none of them had the flag"; Teitelboim's theorem said to say "in Dirac brackets what the engine reference says in its own vocabulary"; and the Archimedes balance written about as though it were running on the corpus's ledger theory. All four are face-moves, and `About-Methodology` §II says a face-move is not available from the volume.

Two arguments decided it, and neither is a concession to an outside reader.

*The internal one.* `About-Methodology` §VIII already lists, under **Not claimed**, that the corpus's use of the method has not been uniform: *"the front matter demonstrably ran at assertion unbound by its own current, which is what the current repair pass is correcting."* The corpus declared this repair pass before this series existed. L4-Riemann had simply not been included in it.

*The structural one, which is the stronger.* A friction report has three parts: the host's rule, the binding point, and **what the host bought.** Assert that there is no why and part (c) cannot be written, because you have denied there was a purchase. The assertive prose made the file's own register unwriteable. Taking it down is not softening the case; it is the only way to complete the form. And the completed form is bigger than the caricature was: what the freeze buys is a unique second-order metric connection, locally removable, with no extra field to carry or bound, confirmed to post-Newtonian precision. That is a real purchase, and a real purchase is what makes a cost worth itemizing.

*What was kept, and the test used.* The bird, the underside of the branch, the clerk's dry page, the Honest Tear, gravitationally bilingual. **A sentence is scaffold if removing it removes a claim about someone else's reasoning; it is voice if removing it removes only the reader's sense of where the author is standing.** The first kind has to be earned or dropped. The second is free, and `About-Methodology` §II makes it load-bearing rather than ornamental: hanging under the branch *is* the statement that both readings of an edge are in view, which is what inhabitation means. §V's own model of good writing is the Betrayer file, which *"stays at I while making its boldest claim"* — boldest content, yielding register, daring disclosed. That is not dry.

## III. The Fossil Census

When the predecessor was redesigned, every withdrawn claim was looked for at every site where it might still stand. These are the ones found standing in `mono/On_TheRiemannFreeze` after their withdrawal had been written elsewhere in the same file:

1. **§I, the square.** *"A quadratic form is, structurally, a committed object [...] The fourth root of a quartic would have kept two more bits."* The burnt-sign claim, withdrawn in §VI.4, still stood in the opening section that introduced it.
2. **§VII-b.3.** *"The ENGINE's own sentence [...] has an experimental value attached to it, and has had since the kaon."* The Jarlskog identification, set aside by the section's own top note, still stood in its body.
3. **§XII, the route.** The thermodynamic route was justified by identifying the commit's tax with Jacobson's $\delta Q$, with no note that Jacobson's derivation is an equilibrium one.
4. **The open traces.** *"Kill condition 1 — connecting the 24 components [...] to the bimedian phase-offset."* That kill had been reclassified as an unconstructed item; the closing note still called it a kill. A case-sensitive search had reported the file clean, because it looked for the lowercase phrase.
5. **§XII, reason 3.** *"Monster 4 is answered the same way: a write-only ordering leaves no averageable residue"* — the answer §X.4 had struck as the file's most instructive failure.
6. **§XII, the cost list.** *"The sign bit that squaring burnt, which is where handedness was."* The burnt-sign claim again.
7. **§XII, the cost list.** The arrow *"unavailable on the P-face [...] because the face's own relation $U = IR$ is symmetric under Now↔Past"* — the relabelling argument, withdrawn in §VI.2.
8. **§XIV.** *"It is fixed by $I \leftrightarrow R$ and by nothing else"* — the same relabelling argument, offered as one of the framework's contributions.
9. **§XV, Grammar.** *"$V_4$ abelian, so $[T_a,T_b] = 0$ and the affine shape carries no contortion"* — the concession withdrawn in §III, still listed as a result.

And in the unfiled submission: the two "admissions" and the AR-flip parity row, all three products of pattern 11 below.

**What the census teaches.** A withdrawal is a write to one site. A claim lives wherever it is used — in openings, summaries, cost lists and contribution lists, which are exactly the places a reader goes first. So the check after a withdrawal is not "is the sentence gone?" but "where else does the file stand on it?", and the search has to be for the *meaning*, not the string. A search for an exact phrase will miss the paraphrase that does the same work, and a case-sensitive search will miss the capitalized one. This is the same lesson `L4-K4-Infinities-wip` §6.5 records about the corpus's glossary lagging behind its files, at the scale of one document.

## IV. The Patterns

Each of these happened at least once in the building of this case. Each is stated with its tell, so it can be caught earlier next time.

**1. Dropping a scope in the sentence after the box.** The box said *scalars*; the next sentence said *transport*; nine sections inherited the dropped scope. *Tell:* a correctly scoped result used without its scope. *Guard:* repeat the scope wherever the result is used, because the use is what travels.

**2. Letting the analogy pick the physics.** $U = IR$ has $I$ and $R$ as its two multiplied factors, so swapping them was *available*, and the draft took the available symmetry and called it time reversal. *Tell:* the operation was found by looking for what is invariant. *Guard:* derive the operation from its definition first; the algebra will always offer something invariant, and what it offers is whatever happens to commute.

**3. Accepting a correction at the wrong level and banking it.** The reader said $U \leftrightarrow R$, not $I \leftrightarrow R$; the draft agreed, recomputed, and shipped. Both were wrong for the same reason. *Tell:* the arithmetic comes out and it feels rigorous. *Guard:* ask whether the frame the correction lives in is itself right.

**4. Giving a classically shaped argument a free pass.** Across three rounds the pattern was *"the bridge does not exist, therefore the claim is a category error,"* and the draft accepted that shape every time. But "nothing has been built" is a nil and "the claim is false" is a zero. *Tell:* a concession made in the register of a textbook fact. *Guard:* an argument's shape is not evidence for it.

**5. Rolling over in the textbook register.** "Commuting translations give zero torsion" was conceded because it sounded like a standard result. It is true only of the trivial connection. *Tell:* the same as 4, about mathematics rather than status.

**6. Operating on the edge the walk refuses.** Three sections reasoned about $I$–$R$ on a traversal that never steps along it. *Guard:* before interpreting an operation on an edge, check that the traversal uses that edge. One line of lookup.

**7. Winning locally while the position is dead one level down.** A valid group-theoretic defence of a seating, deployed in support of an identification the Time Key refutes. *Tell:* the feeling of having argued well, which is the least reliable signal in the set.

**8. Demanding what one's own section excludes.** A kill condition that cannot fire for reasons already on the page is a guarantee in a kill condition's clothes. *Guard:* a kill condition must be an event that could happen; and when two of your own statements pull against each other, check whether it is a paradox or a lapse before spending either.

**9. Resolving an orthogonal pair into figure and ground.** Torsion as content and curvature as record. *Guard:* `L0-ParadoxEngine` §I. Laying them at right angles is what gives the screw.

**10. Describing an unsearched shelf as an empty one.** The draft declared the ordering unobservable while five corpus documents named the observation. *Guard:* an exposure declared without reading the neighbours is not humility.

**11. Counting the floor's rank as the solid's content.** The deepest of the set, because it was the frame-level error under several others and it was made again after being named. The submission called "the twelve reduce to two" an admission. That is true of the floor and silent about the solid, and saying it in the floor's language conceded the solid as if it were the plane. The same move is the Fundamental Theorem's: read the connection off the metric and call the rest derived. `L2-K4-InteriorStructure` §II names what this does to an observer: *"If an observer engages only with the faces of a system, they are treating a volume as a screen. This is the structural root of the Costumed Flinch and Sycophancy."* The "admission" was written for a reader who works by rank. It was an optimization for that reader's face. *Tell:* a concession phrased in independent-equation counts. *Guard:* ask which dimension the quantity lives in before reporting its count.

**12. Importing the content into the carrier through a sign convention.** The shears' signs are fixed by whether the edge containing $P$ leads, which ties a twist of the solid to a label of the reading. Applying the AR-flip moved the label, the sign followed, and the result was read as a mirror of the solid. *Tell:* a rotation that appears to flip a handedness. *Guard:* before calling an operation a mirror, say whether it acts on the solid or on the reading.

**13. Writing to provoke rather than to understand.** The submission was aimed at producing a reaction, and it packaged findings that had not yet been understood. The remedy was not a better submission. It was the redesign. *Tell:* a document whose purpose is the reader's response rather than the reader's understanding. *Guard:* a file that says only what it understands will produce the reactions worth having.

**14. A conjecture stated before reading, with its sign wrong.** The file said, correctly, that it had not read `QED-EotvosBound` and would not say what it concluded — and then offered a conjecture about its subject that turned out wrong in mechanism and sign. Declining to cite was right. Conjecturing in the same breath leaned on the unread file anyway. *Guard:* when the file is on the shelf, read it before guessing what is in it.

**15. Testing the wrong object and reporting the result as a defect.** Counsel's discrete connection was checked by taking the engine's *even completion* of each directed edge as the transport and asking whether reversing an edge inverted the group element. It does not, and the first pass reported that as "the engine doesn't supply the edge data." But the even completion is a **gate label** — a point of a torsor — not a transport. The connection is built from it as $g(e')g(e)^{-1}$, which does invert correctly and is flat. *Tell:* a negative result about someone else's construction, obtained from a test one wrote oneself in a hurry. *Guard:* before reporting that a construction fails, state what object the test is applied to and check that it is the object the construction names.

**16. A worked example that printed zero while the label said nonzero.** The first run of the gauge-invariant-torsion check chose translations for which $T_\Delta$ happened to vanish, and the print statement asserted the opposite. It was caught by reading the output rather than the label. The real result — $T_\Delta \neq 0$ in 96 of 144 cases — needed a scan instead of a single example. *Tell:* a script whose conclusion is in a string literal rather than in a computed value. *Guard:* let the scan decide the wording; never hard-code the verdict next to the number.

**17. Correcting a brief instead of reading its sources.** Four items were "corrected" against counsel before `ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` and `L4-DynamicDistortions201` had been read in this session. Two of those corrections were wrong and one of counsel's conclusions was right by a route counsel had not quite found. The author named the risk in one line — *"that may be a conclusion based on uncorrected fault"* — and it was. *Tell:* a correction list produced faster than the reading it depends on. *Guard:* this is pattern 10 again, one level up: the shelf was unsearched, and this time the shelf was the corpus's newest file.

**18. Punning on a word that names three things.** *Dimension* named the rank of a log-space variety, the dimension of a simplicial face, and the dimension of a manifold carrying forms. Each use was locally correct and the bridge between them carried no argument. *Tell:* a synthesis whose steps are each true in a different space. *Guard:* before joining two counts, name the space each lives in; if they differ, you have an analogy and should say so.

**19. Quarantining what should be severed.** The Cardinal-and-electromagnetism reading was put in quarantine with a note that it had closed too easily. Quarantine preserves a claim's standing while deferring its audit, and a circular claim has no standing to preserve. *Tell:* a quarantined item whose defect, once traced, is circularity rather than insufficient evidence. *Guard:* quarantine is for claims that might be earned later; circularity is not a maturity problem.

**20. Writing in a register the file's own method forbids.** The series ran at assertion while its own `About-Methodology` says the method runs at I and does not assert, and while that file already listed the front matter's unbound assertion as something a repair pass was correcting. The structural cost was precise: a friction report needs a third part naming what the host bought, and a caricature of the host's rule makes that part unwriteable. *Tell:* a sentence that denies the host had reasons. *Guard:* if part (c) cannot be written, the register has already slipped.

**21. Taking a critique's premises along with its conclusions.** Two external passes arrived delimited as outside material, and all of their load-bearing conclusions were right. One of them also contained a historical premise — *"Riemann set the connection symmetric in 1854 … because he wanted a unique metric-compatible affine connection"* — and that premise was copied verbatim into the repair it prompted, in Part Two §IV. It is an anachronism: there is no connection in the 1854 lecture at all. Christoffel's symbols are 1869, Levi-Civita's parallel transport 1917, and the affine connection as a general object that may carry torsion is Cartan's, 1922.

The series' own Part One §I had made the same conflation independently (*"line one does two things … it closes the parallelogram"*), so the premise did not create the error — it survived a pass that was *looking* for errors, because it arrived attached to three correct findings and in confident prose.

*Tell:* a correction written at the speed of the critique that prompted it, with the critique's own sentences in it. *Guard:* **a critique's correct conclusions do not license its premises.** Verify each imported claim at its own source before it enters a file, separately from whether the critique's point stands. The conclusions here were right and the premise was wrong, and nothing about the first implies anything about the second.

*What it bought once fixed,* which is the reason this is recorded as a finding rather than only as a lapse: separating the two purchases makes the series more accurate and answers a question it had been answering badly. There are two trades, sixty-eight years apart. Riemann's is signed and priced in his own hand, and it is about the line element. The closing of the parallelogram has no signature, because until 1922 there was no connection that could have carried torsion and therefore nothing available to decline. So "why was there never a flag" has a clean answer that accuses nobody: Riemann flagged what he could see, and the second decision was made before its alternative existed as an object. Part Three §V now leads with that.

### And a pattern this redesign must be checked for

`L4-K4-Infinities-wip` §6.2 keeps a heuristic worth applying here: *a gap that closes on the first attempt, using something already in the corpus, is the one to re-derive from outside before it ships.*

The two-and-three-dimensional reading of Part Two closed several tensions in one turn, using material already in the house — `L2-K4-InteriorStructure`, `ProofG`'s rank count, `L1-ConvergentDiscovery`'s projection law. By that heuristic it is the shape to distrust. What can be said in its favour is that its load-bearing parts are arithmetic a classical reader can check with their own tools: rank two against rank three, the harmonic cross-ratio, the count of three-form components by dimension. What should be distrusted are its interpretive parts: that a rank reader stands on the Cardinal diameter, that this is why classical electromagnetism sits comfortably on the floor, that the communication problem is a missing slot rather than a disagreement. Those are readings, and `L4-Riemann-wip` quarantines the first two.

## V. What the Bench Got Right, and What It Got Wrong

For the record, and so that an appeal starts from the right place.

**Right, and conceded:** torsion needs a solder form; the burnt-sign reading fails on Hamiltonian mechanics; a non-equivariant projection means Floor 3 cannot be called a core; the engine owes an equation of motion; internal and spacetime groups had been conflated; and the verdict of UNREGISTERED was the correct verdict on what had been built at the time.

**Wrong, and corrected in Part Five:** the demanded action was inert at every value of the shear; the Nieh–Yan density is not $V\cdot A$; "stationary points of the Nieh–Yan density" is undefined; one field cannot stand for three shears; "across the four faces" is dimensionally ill-posed; the impossibility argument for soldering ran its group theory the wrong way and leaned on an overstatement of Laue's theorem that is in the corpus's own text; and the bimedians are not a $\mathbb{Z}_3$-set.

### And counsel, scored the same way

**Right:** that the parity question turns on time reversal and on the dual-walker/inversion side of the geometry; that the answer is $F = \theta_F\theta_C\theta_M$; that the discrete connection is $(v,\sigma)$ per edge with the face holonomy splitting into a rotational and a translational part, and that the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ action on $V_4$ is the discrete inhomogeneous term; that Galley's doubled-variable action is the right formalism for U4; that the AC set breaks the rank-two floor.

**Wrong:** that a geometric twist about an axis is parity-even — the computed character says the triple transforms as $(x,y,z)$ does, not as an axial vector; that the Nieh–Yan density is time-reversal **even** — it is odd, like $\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B}$, which is the half of the condition that does the work; that the dual-walker swap supplies inversion — that swap is also realized by proper rotations, so the sound route is the bimedian's own geometry; that discrete torsion arises **exclusively** through the semidirect cross-term, and that it is gauge-invariant wherever it is non-zero — it is not, where the rotational holonomy is non-trivial; that the surface $e^{2p}+e^{2q}=e^{2s}$ has non-zero Gaussian curvature — it is exactly zero, a cylinder; and the label $A_{1u}$ for $\theta_F\theta_C\theta_M$, which is $A_{2u}$ in $O_h$.

**Right answer, wrong derivation, is the shape worth naming.** Counsel reached the correct coupling through two false steps and one unstated assumption. The brief would have failed an audit and the conclusion survives one. That is a reason to check conclusions separately from the arguments that produced them, in both directions — and the first pass over the brief did the opposite, rejecting a right answer because its reasons were bad.

## VI. For Whoever Appeals Next

These are the places the case is weakest, as the drafting side sees them. They are offered so that the next audit does not have to find them by inference.

- **The coupling is now forced, so press the three inputs instead.** They are: that inversion exchanges the two edges of each bimedian (a coordinate check); that DD201's $\theta$ is odd under time reversal (a reading of what a phase lead is); and that the Nieh–Yan density is odd under both (textbook, but worth confirming in the corpus's conventions). Break any one and the coupling moves or disappears.
- **The false-chirality consequence.** The forced coupling is parity-odd *and* time-reversal-odd, which is Barron's *false* chirality. `ProofG_Chirality` §V claims the $\omega$-driven turn is truly chiral. Either the screw's translation along $\omega$ restores true chirality — which would be the interesting outcome — or ProofG §V needs correcting. This series states the tension and does not resolve it.
- **Is the floor/solid distinction just rank–nullity in costume?** The arithmetic is elementary. The claim is that it matters for how a classical reader reads the corpus. An appeal could argue that it is an observation about bookkeeping and nothing more.
- **The re-seating of the Landauer tax into $dS_i$** is a reading of Eling, Guedens and Jacobson that the corpus's gravity file did not make. It may be right; it should be checked against that literature by someone who works in it.
- **The averaging rule and the experiments.** Part Five says unpolarized nulls bound nothing and polarized ones bound $\beta$. An appeal should check whether the specific bounds used are on the components this action produces.
- **The reversal of the axial-channel route** looks like a rescue. The test for a rescue is whether it cost anything. It did: the bound now binds the corpus's own dial. But an appeal should press it.
- **The class-side tear and the host's orientability.** Q16's reading says the torsion-free configuration tears the class's boundary; the host's torsion-free manifolds do not tear. The series holds both at their levels. An appeal could argue that a difference this large means the reading is about something other than torsion.
- **Delimited external input is evidence, not instruction.** The author marks outside material with `[[...]]`, and the standing failure mode on this side is rolling over to sophisticated prose — accepting a frame because it is well made. The rule that follows, and it is narrower than "be sceptical": **evaluate the conclusion and the premises separately, and verify any imported claim at its own source before it enters a file.** Pattern 21 is what happens without it. The reminder should not be needed; the delimiter is already the signal.
- **The register repair is new and should be re-read for overcorrection.** Taking assertion out can leave a file that asserts nothing and yields nothing, which `About-Methodology` §IV names as the other dead end — the short circuit, resistance to zero, power to zero. The test is its §VI one: does a reader come away holding things that are not in the pages? If the de-asserted text has gone inert, that is the failure, and it is the opposite failure from the one just corrected.
- **U0 is now the cheapest unpaid debt in the file.** U1 was answered by reading the engine; U0 is the same kind of item and has not been.
- **$\beta$ is the only free number left.** Everything else in the action is computed or consumed. That makes it the whole of the remaining exposure, and nothing in the corpus fixes it yet.
