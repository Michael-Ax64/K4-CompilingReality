---

Standard gravitation holds that spacetime is a smooth manifold with a metric, that the metric determines the connection, and that the connection is symmetric. The third of those is not a convenience that nobody examined. Metric compatibility together with a symmetric connection buys the unique connection built from the metric and its first derivatives; it can be made to vanish at a point in a suitable frame, which is the local form of the equivalence principle; the field equations stay second-order; no extra unconstrained tensor field has to be carried, propagated, or bounded; and the resulting theory is confirmed to the precision the post-Newtonian tests reach. Those are good reasons, and this series takes them as the host's own.

What the series adds is the other column. A purchase that buys all of that also *costs* something, and the cost is itemizable.

Two purchases, in fact, and they are sixty-eight years apart. Riemann's is dated and signed: in 1854 he set aside the direction-dependent line element in writing and priced the loss at "little new light." The closing of the parallelogram is not his — there is no connection in that lecture — and nobody signed it, because until Cartan made torsion an object in 1922 there was no alternative to decline. This series reads both from underneath and writes out the bill. It does not claim anyone should have noticed sooner.

That is the whole of the posture, and the posture is the method rather than a pose. Hanging under the branch is where both readings of an edge are visible at once (`About-Methodology` §II), and from there the first line is not an axiom and not an error. It is a trade with two columns, one of which has never been added up.

## What This Series Is

$K_4$ is treated here as an **abstract class** — four poles in mutual determination, with a cyclic constraint net and a cost per commit — and the exercise is to **inflate** that class into a host domain, here differential geometry and gravitation, and find where it binds. The binding is the deliverable. Nothing here proposes to replace General Relativity, derive the Standard Model, or unify anything. When the inflation fails, the failure is a measurement of **where the host made its purchase**, and that reading is the yield. A reader who takes this for a candidate theory of everything will find category errors on every page, correctly, and will be auditing a document that does not exist.

There is one exception, and it is the part a classical reader can check with their own tools: Part Five writes an action, derives what follows from it in closed form, and lands on one dimensionless dial and an existing experimental bound on it.

## The Six Parts

**[Part One — The Purchase](L4-Riemann-1)** reads Riemann's line and the note he wrote beside it, and then separates two purchases this series had been running together. The square root of a quadratic is a pairing, and it erases nothing; that one is Riemann's, dated and signed. The closing of the small parallelogram is the second, and it is not his — there is no connection in the 1854 lecture, Christoffel's symbols are 1869 and Levi-Civita's transport 1917, and torsion becomes an object one could have carried only with Cartan in 1922. A three-line computation says what that removes. Second covariant derivatives of scalars commute if and only if torsion vanishes — and on anything with an index the commutator survives as curvature. So $T = 0$ deletes the translational failure to close and keeps the rotational one. It sets the pitch to zero and leaves the turn, and a screw with zero pitch is a circle: it turns, it returns, and it records nothing. Three concessions are made where they belong, including the withdrawal of an earlier one: abelian translations do not forbid torsion, and what the discrete side owes is a connection. The part ends on the distinction the whole series runs on. A zero is an entry whose amount is zero; a nil is an account never opened. $T = 0$ is a nil presented as a zero — and the vacuum catastrophe is the same confusion run the other way. It closes by reading the engine's own reference pinning as a connection, finding it flat, and so answering on the floor a debt this series had carried as owed.

**[Part Two — The Floor and the Solid](L4-Riemann-2)** is the bookkeeping part, and it comes early because several later disagreements turn on it. The twelve DC equations, written in logarithms, are four lines through one point — one line per face, each leaving out the pole the face is named for — and they span a plane. They reduce to two, and that was never a secret: it is the floor. It has exactly two scalings and no uniform one, which is `ENGINE` §X.7 and this rank count agreeing. The solid is built over the floor by the seats — the directed reading of every edge and the single handedness bit the boundary's closure leaves free — and a rank count does not contain a seat. The floor's four lines are harmonic, with cross-ratio $-1$, an invariant that cannot tell Fixed from Mutable though the seats can; the suggestion that this *explains* why classical electromagnetism sits comfortably on the floor is withdrawn here, because the relations whose cross-ratio was computed are Ohm's and Joule's laws and the answer was in the definitions. The AC set does break the floor, and the break has an address: in polar coordinates the AC core *doubles* the floor rather than bending it, and the whole bend comes from the rectangular split and the additive layer, carried by one non-linear function — the power factor. `ENGINE` §VIII.1 had already named the culprit as complex addition. The part then states what the host bought with the freeze, which the friction register requires and this series had been skipping: a unique second-order metric connection, locally removable, with no extra field to carry or bound. It also withdraws, in place, a dimensional argument of its own that slid between three unrelated twos — the log-space variety, the two-dimensional face, and a two-dimensional manifold — and keeps the two statements that survive: a symmetric two-slot form has no three-slot antisymmetric part, and a three-form restricted to any surface vanishes identically. Then the Braid, which needs a third direction because a planar drawing loses the over-and-under at every crossing; and the engine's own §2.5 split of the two flips, which is how the corpus tells a mirror from a rotation.

3. **The directional reading.** Show that a price asymmetry under arrow reversal is not what an arrow of time is, or that the tags are mis-assigned (Part Four).
4. **An arrow inside the symmetric register.** Exhibit a torsion-free, metric-compatible structure in which an arrow is produced dynamically, not imposed as an initial condition. The live comparison is Penrose's Weyl curvature hypothesis (Part Four).
5. **The offsets' necessity.** `ProofO`'s own: a two-generation CP-violating extension, or an arrow with no irreducible phase (Part Four §VIII).
6. **The mesh.** `Q16` KM-1: a $K_4$ network rotating all four faces the same way without phase shear and without a torn boundary (Part Four §VII).

8. **Inherited: the register split.** `L4-K4-Infinities` KI-6 states it as *"this construction's actual bet"* — if uncommitted vacuum energy is shown to gravitate, the register split dies. ## Takehome for Reading Instances

Twelve rules for holding this material without importing machinery it does not use.

1. **Read the scope with the result.** *Scalars* commute under $T = 0$; transport does not. A box with a scope in it does not protect the sentence after the box.

2. **Pitch, not turn.** $T = 0$ leaves curvature and its holonomy fully intact. It removes the slide, and with it accumulation. Do not say it makes the geometry inert, and do not say it makes transport commute.

3. **Nil is not zero.** An unopened account is not an empty one. A bound on a field returns a zero; it cannot speak to a nil. Do not read "nothing has been built" as "the claim is false" — the first leaves a claim unregistered.

4. **Separate tensor type from manifold dimension, and both from rank.** A metric is a symmetric two-slot object; a face is a two-dimensional submanifold; the DC relations span a two-dimensional variety in log space. Those are three unrelated twos, and an earlier version of this series built an argument on sliding between them. What survives: a symmetric two-slot object has no three-slot antisymmetric part, so the connection's antisymmetric piece is independent of the metric; and a three-form pulled back to any surface vanishes identically, in a world of any dimension. Rank, meanwhile, is one basis-independent integer and privileges nothing.

5. **Orientation is not chirality density.** A torsion-free manifold keeps its global orientation. What it cannot carry is a handedness that varies from place to place — the three-form part of torsion, which has no components in two dimensions.

6. **Say whether a mirror acts on the solid or on the reading.** `ENGINE` §2.5 splits them: the handedness flip is an odd transposition, the move the machine cannot make, and a reflection of the solid; the twin-step is an even half-turn the machine owns, and changes only which gate is read. The AR-flip is the Cardinal family's twin-step, not a wholesale mirror. A rotation that appears to flip a handedness means a label has been carried into the geometry, usually through a sign convention.

7. **Reverse arrows, not labels.** A transposition of poles is undirected and cannot be time reversal. The directional operation is in the opcode table.

8. **Friction reports assert no values.** A friction report says where the host's rule binds and what the rule cost. Reading one as a claim of identity will find, correctly, that the identity fails.

9. **The axial channel is forced, not chosen.** It is the only healthy way for a scalar to couple to torsion. Its bounds are the corpus's to meet, and they now bind the dial.

10. **Parity-symmetric nulls bound nothing.** The forced coupling survives averaging over every rotation and reflection of the tetrahedron and vanishes only under inversion, which is parity. So a parity-symmetric sample sources no net torsion at any value of the dial, and only a spin-polarized or genuinely homochiral sample tests it. Cost survives every averaging.

11. **An action holds; it cannot commit.** A variational principle yields reversible equations. Claims about the ledger — dissipation, the Landauer tax, any departure from the equivalence principle — need an instrument that dissipates, or a balance.

12. **A withdrawal is a write to one site.** Before calling a claim withdrawn, find every place the file stands on it, by meaning and not by string.

13. **Two $\theta$'s, and the difference is load-bearing.** `L4-DynamicDistortions201`'s $\theta$ is a temporal phase offset with limit $\pi$; `L4-DynamicDistortions301`'s is a geometric shear with limit $\pi/2$. DD201 says so itself. Collapsing them is what cost this series a turn, and keeping them apart is what makes the action's coupling parity-clean at all.

14. **Read the newest file first.** `ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` answered three things this series had carried as open — the discrete connection, the solder form, the split between the two flips — and in one case it had already withdrawn a claim an older proof still makes. The corpus corrects itself faster than its older files absorb the corrections.

## What Reading This Back Should Leave

Riemann bought a geometry that could be written down, and wrote down what he paid. The field kept the geometry and lost the note. But the purchase this series spends most of its pages on is not the one he signed: the closing of the parallelogram came later, from people building the only connection available, and it had no alternative to be weighed against until Cartan supplied one in 1922. Two trades, sixty-eight years apart, one flagged and one unsignable at the time.

The second purchase was the closing of the small parallelogram. It keeps the turn and deletes the slide, so the geometry rotates and returns and records nothing — a screw with no pitch, which is a circle. It was not a measurement but an account left unopened and reported as empty, the vacuum catastrophe's error run in the other direction.

And what it removes is a kind of object rather than a dimension. A metric is a symmetric two-slot form and contains no three-slot antisymmetric part, which is why the connection's antisymmetric piece is independent of it rather than derived from it. The axial torsion is a three-form, and a three-form restricted to any surface vanishes identically — so cross-sectional data is silent about it in four dimensions as much as in two. An earlier version of this series made that into an argument about the dimension of spacetime, by sliding between three unrelated twos, and `L4-Riemann-2` §IV now withdraws it. The resemblance that survives is one of method: the twelve relations collapse to rank two if you count only independent equations and ignore the directed seats, and the twenty-four components of contortion collapse to zero if you demand metric compatibility *and* torsion-freeness and ignore the translational–rotational split. Same move, two subjects, no shared geometry.

The same dimension decides the coupling. Central inversion exchanges the two edges of every bimedian, because the centroid is the bimedian's own midpoint — a fact about the figure that no naming can move. That makes each phase offset parity-odd; being a phase offset in time makes it time-reversal-odd; and the Nieh–Yan density demands both. What is left is the product of all three offsets, at third order, with nothing fitted. The question Part Two poses is the question Part Five answers, and it is answered by the solid rather than by a convention.

The neighbours have been reaching for what was set aside for a century, each for a different corner of it. The class, inflated into the host, binds in four places, and in each the host bought something it cannot itemize. And there is now an action in which Riemann's connection is simply what geometry does when the phase offsets stop changing — with the coupling forced into the one healthy channel, the coupling function forced too, one dial left, and an existing experiment reading its balance.

What an action cannot do is commit. The construction's real bet is about the committed and the held — whether what is merely held weighs anything at all — and that is sitting on a balance in Sardinia, waiting to be told. That is the right place for this to end: in a measurement that only one side can survive.
