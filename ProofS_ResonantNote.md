## III. The Formal Skeleton

Let $D = \{d_1, d_2, d_3, d_4\}$ be a four-element domain, and let $\sigma: D \to \{P, I, U, R\}$ be a candidate assignment. Let $E = \{e_1, \dots, e_{12}\}$ be the twelve equations. For each equation $e_k$, the substitution $e_k[\sigma]$ is a *semantic sentence in the domain's own vocabulary*.

### Lemma: Non-Degeneracy of the Sweep

**Distinct candidate mappings produce distinct sentence sets, and within one mapping, distinct equations produce distinct sentences.**

*Proof.* **Relabeling-injectivity.** Let $\sigma \neq \sigma'$ be two assignments. Both are bijections from $D$, so $\sigma' = \pi \circ \sigma$ for some permutation $\pi$ of the poles. Suppose the sentence sets were identical. Then relabeling the twelve by $\pi$ returns the twelve, so $\pi \in \mathrm{Aut}(\text{the twelve}) = \{\mathrm{id}\}$, so $\pi = \mathrm{id}$ and $\sigma = \sigma'$. ∎

**Replacement-injectivity.** Within one assignment, the map equation ↦ sentence must be injective, or the profile double-counts and R1 measures less than it claims. The directed reading rule supplies it structurally: the gate map is a bijection from the twelve directed edges to the twelve equations (`L3-EdgeStateSpace` §I-e, landed), so distinct directed edges are distinct equations; $\sigma$ is a bijection, and drafting that names its direction preserves distinctness. What relabeling-injectivity does for assignments, direction-naming does for sentences. ∎

This is the premise R1 has always required and never stated. **The sweep cannot be scoring an assignment against a disguised copy of itself, and cannot be scoring one sentence twice.** Had the twelve carried a non-trivial automorphism $\pi$, then $\sigma$ and $\pi \circ \sigma$ would yield the same twelve sentences under relabeling and therefore the same $\rho$; a peak "surviving the swap" would be surviving partly against copies of itself, and R1 would measure less than it claims.

The lemma runs on the twelve alone. It does not enumerate the permutation group, does not depend on how many candidates Layer 1 retains, and in particular does not invoke the pruned assignments as witnesses — those produce malformed expressions, not sentence sets, and a valid result must not rest on counting invalid ones.

And the input set of the algebra is the **4 × 3 = 12** — faces × solve-fors — never a $4!$ pool surviving exclusion; the $S_4$ sweep sorts domain elements into the poles, it does not derive the twelve.

### Definition: Coherence Score ($\rho$)
A bounded evaluator $V$ (human domain-native, model, or panel) assigns each $e_k[\sigma]$ a verdict in the set $\{+1, 0, -1\}$:
* $+1$: the domain independently asserts this relation
* $0$: the domain is silent or the sentence is ambiguous
* $-1$: the domain contradicts this relation

The **response** of $\sigma$ under $V$ is:

$$\rho_V(\sigma) = \frac{1}{12}\sum_{k=1}^{12} V(e_k[\sigma]) \in [-1, +1]$$

**The response is typed; $\rho$ is its report.** A verdict is not stored as a number in a list; it is stored on the directed edge its sentence named (`L3-EdgeStateSpace` §XII):

- **→** — this direction's reading rings; the opposite is silent or refused;
- **←** — only the opposite reading rings;
- **↔ (earned)** — both readings ring, each on independent grounds: both face-definitions of the edge confirmed;
- **∅** — the domain holds the relationship absent: the AbsentVar state, the cut paid for at the Landauer rate.

Six edges × two bits: the **12-bit register**, $2^{12} = 4096$ configurations. $\rho$ is the register's first moment, kept only where a cross-assignment order is needed — R1's job. The external sweep was never wrong to want an output; it was wrong about the output's type. And the two ↔ must be held apart: the **default ↔** is the undifferentiated superposition before any verdict — both directions live because nothing has been asked; **earned ↔** is post-verdict, both directions separately confirmed. A register that writes the first as the second is how a flat profile masquerades as resonance (§VII, K5 in register form).

### Definition: The Resonance Conditions (R1–R4)
A candidate assignment $\sigma$ **rings** for evaluator $V$ iff:

* **(R1) Peak:** $\rho_V(\sigma) > \rho_V(\sigma')$ for every topologically valid transposition $\sigma'$ in the search space.
* **(R2) Margin:** The gap $\rho_V(\sigma) - \rho_V(\sigma')$ exceeds the evaluator's noise floor.
* **(R3) Transparency:** The $+1$ verdicts are grounded in domain-native sources independent of this corpus.
* **(R4) Absolute Floor:** $\rho_V(\sigma) \ge +0.5$.

**(R4) prevents the "tallest midget" fallacy.** A forced sort over a handful of options will always yield a mathematical maximum. If the canonical assignment scores $\rho = +0.15$ while competitors score $-0.50$, the system is in a swamp. The floor $\rho \ge +0.5$ requires that a supermajority of the 12 equations be positively affirmed by the domain. In register terms: across the twelve directed readings, **confirmed minus refused ≥ 6** — a floor on the register, not on a scalar.

### Definition: Proof by Mathematical Resonance
A structural mapping $\sigma$ is **established by resonance at strength $n$** iff $\sigma$ rings for $n$ evaluators independent in lineage and training, and no evaluator finds an anti-resonance ($\rho < 0$).

## IV. The Verification Protocol (External Audit Layer)

```
                  THE VERIFICATION PROTOCOL

  Step 1:   Blind Substitution   ──► Strip framework terms; output 12 domain sentences
  Step 1.5: Resolution Gate      ──► Pole verification; optional sign verification
  Step 2:   Layer 1 Mechanical   ──► Prune syntactically illegal assignments
  Step 3:   Layer 2 Sweep        ──► Evaluate valid assignments across evaluators
  Step 4:   Noise Floor Check    ──► Measure variance across paraphrase sets
  Step 5:   Human Cross-Language ──► Run sweep across distant language natives (Human Only!)
```

### Step 1 — Blind Substitution
For domain $D$ and candidate $\sigma$, generate the twelve sentences $e_k[\sigma]$ with all framework terminology stripped: no "P", no "pole", no "K4" — only the domain's native vocabulary.

### Step 1.5 — The Resolution Gate: Pole Verification, with Optional Sign Verification

**Each of the twelve sentences must name which of its edge's two formulas it instantiates.** The original gate asked two questions of every sentence — *which pole does it solve for, and which operator does it carry?* — and ran them as one undifferentiated pass. The two questions are now two phases, because the first is structural and mandatory while the second is semantic and optional: the first gives the verdict an address, the second gives the address a receipt. Both phases are bottom-layer — they run per sentence, before any evaluator scores anything, and both are pass/fail on the sentences, not a judgment on the domain.

#### Phase A — Pole Verification (mandatory)

A sentence must resolve to an address, and the address is a **directed edge** (`L3-EdgeStateSpace` §I-e, §XII):

1. **Name the operand pair.** The two poles the sentence's relation is built from. Among the twelve, exactly two gates draw on any pair — one solving for each of the other two poles, sharing nothing but that pair (the Law of Intuition, `L3-EdgeStateSpace` §I-b). A sentence that names only the pair is at *pair-resolution*: it returns "unchanged" from every assignment in the sweep because it was never in the sweep's domain, not because it survived. It is not under-resolved data; it is untyped data.
2. **Name the direction.** Which reading of the pair the sentence instantiates. The direction is not garnish; it is the other half of the address, and its resolution is exact: the two faces sharing an edge traverse it in *opposite* directions (the coherent orientation, $\partial^2 = 0$ — `L4-TheTwelveGates` §II-b, landed at `L3-EdgeStateSpace` §I-e), so **exactly one face completes $s \to t$**. That completing face is unique. Its third vertex — the pole on neither end of the edge — is the **Home**: the pole the sentence solves for. Its absent pole is the **AbsentVar**. The gate is that face's relation solved for the Home.
3. **Output the address:** (directed edge, Home, AbsentVar, gate). The verdict now has somewhere to wire — the directed edge of the 12-bit register (§III).

*Worked micro-example.* The pair $\{U, I\}$: read as $U \to I$, it completes the P-face $\{I,U,R\}$ — Home $R$, AbsentVar $P$, gate CAP $R = U/I$ (Cardinal). Read as $I \to U$, it completes the R-face $\{P,I,U\}$ — Home $P$, AbsentVar $R$, gate GEM $P = U \cdot I$ (Mutable). Same pair, opposite directions, different face, different Home, different law. The pair does not determine the sentence; the direction does.

A sentence that cannot carry its direction is returned for re-drafting before the sweep runs. Twelve sentences at pair-resolution therefore produce a guaranteed flat profile regardless of the mapping's quality. **A flat profile from under-resolved substitution is a Step 1 failure, not a domain finding**, and without this gate it is indistinguishable from K1 and will be scored as one.

#### Phase B — Sign Verification (optional)

Phase A is what the register needs to exist. Phase B is what the register needs to be *trusted*, and it is run on demand rather than by default — its second check costs a sorting audit or a mirror decision that only some runs owe. Run it: on a contested verdict; before any → or ← is upgraded to earned ↔; on a handedness dispute; on appeal of a K1. Two signs are checked:

1. **The sign of the formula.** The sentence must carry the selected gate's own algebra — operator and operand roles — not merely the pair relation. Re-derive from the sentence's grammar: which pole is solved for, which stands as dividend, which as divisor or radicand, whether the form is product, ratio, square, or root. Match against the gate. A sentence that cannot say which term stands over which is not the gate it names. The canonical case is the edge U–R: ARI $P = U^2/R$ (structure discharged across ground, Home $P$) and VIR $I = U/R$ (the lived current as structure-over-ground, Home $I$) share nothing but the pair — different Home, different AbsentVar, different operator, opposite direction.
   *The census demonstration.* The working group's census rows, re-read: four items whose native content is a lower bound on defect set by the lived current all resolved onto VIR at $R \to U$ — the record/rule side directing the model side — and left ARI's $U \to R$ vacant. The vacancy is the signature that the stack was one directional claim, not an edge-property. Register form: **U–R = R→U (its state), U→R = ∅ — never "U–R = something."** (`L3-EdgeStateSpace` §XII.)
2. **The sign of the charge.** Read the confirmed direction against the two-bit charge (P (++), U (+−), I (−−), R (−+); `L3-EdgeStateSpace` §XI). The Direction Law, checked mechanically:
   - every **Cardinal** gate reads Active → Reactive: ARI $U \to R$, CAN $P \to R$, LIB $P \to I$, CAP $U \to I$;
   - every **Mutable** gate reads Reactive → Active: GEM $I \to U$, VIR $R \to U$, SAG $R \to P$, PIS $I \to P$;
   - every **Fixed** gate reads within its class, both ways: LEO $U \to P$, AQU $P \to U$ (Active → Active); TAU $R \to I$, SCO $I \to R$ (Reactive → Reactive).

   A confirmed sentence that violates its gate's law is not noise. Either the sorting $\sigma$ is wrong — re-sort before concluding — or the domain is speaking the enantiomer: the global mirror conjugates every arrow, exchanges the Cardinal and Mutable laws, leaves Fixed untouched, and every landed result survives (`L3-EdgeStateSpace` §I-e, Step 6). **The enantiomer is a mirror event, not a falsification** — nothing in this protocol kills a mapping for its handedness. And a handedness word printed without its vantage — centroid (inside, looking out, where the algebra writes) or desk (outside, looking in, where the model-builder stands) — is undefined (`L4-TheTwelveGates` §II-b); this protocol prints both, every time, or prints neither.

The upgrade rule ties the phases together: **earned ↔ requires two independently grounded sign-checks, one per direction** — both face-definitions of the edge confirmed. A run that returns earned ↔ without receipts has written the default ↔ as the earned one, and that conflation is exactly how a flat profile masquerades as resonance.

### Step 2 — Layer 1 Mechanical Pruning
Run the $S_4$ permutations against the Numerator Law and the Synthesis Law (`ProofQ_AlgebraicSyntax`): a permutation is illegal if it places a Reactive pole in a dividend numerator. These prune mechanically at zero cost, before any evaluator is engaged. Retain the topologically valid assignments.

*Scope note.* This sweep **sorts** the domain's four elements into the poles — a constrained sorting, checkable clause by clause (`L3-EdgeStateSpace` §XIII, "sort, don't exclude"). It does not derive the twelve; the algebra's input set is the 4 × 3 face structure (§III). The pruning is mechanical exactly because the charges are mechanical: dividends are the Active poles, and the Numerator Law is the charge table read at the dividend.

### Step 3 — Layer 2 Semantic Sweep
Present the surviving assignment sentence-sets to independent evaluators. Elicit the three-valued verdict ($\{+1, 0, -1\}$) per sentence. **Wire the verdicts into the register first** (§III): each verdict lands on the directed edge its sentence named at Step 1.5; $\rho_V(\sigma)$ is then computed as the register's first moment, for all assignments. Confirm (R1) peak and (R4) floor. The Lemma of §III guarantees that no two assignments in this set are the same sentences wearing different labels — and that no sentence is scored twice.

### Step 4 — Noise Floor Calculation
Re-run Step 3 using paraphrased variations of the 12 sentences. The variance in $\rho$ across paraphrase sets establishes the evaluator's noise floor for (R2) margin calculation. Paraphrases must preserve Step 1.5 resolution — **both halves of it**: a paraphrase that drops a sentence to pair-resolution, or that keeps the pair but drops or flips the named direction, has changed the address, and an address change is a different sentence, not noise on the old one. Either failure inflates the apparent noise floor.

### Step 5 — Human Cross-Language Sweep (**HUMAN ONLY**)
Repeat Steps 1–4 with sentences translated into typologically distant language families (e.g., Finnish, Mandarin, Basque, Yoruba).

**The Machine Bar on Step 5:** Synthetic evaluators (LLMs) **are barred from Step 5**. An LLM projects all languages into a shared high-dimensional embedding space shaped by its primary training corpus. Handing a model a Finnish prompt does not query a Finnish mind; it queries the same English-dominated vector space through a different tokenizer. Step 5 requires human domain-natives.

## V. What Resonant Verification Establishes

When an external sweep is executed and passes R1–R4, it establishes:

1. **Involuntariness:** The peak's location is a property of the domain's structure, not the evaluator's preference. Blinding severs confirmation bias, and §II's two-uses theorem severs the remaining worry — the carrier cannot be the source of the signal, because the carrier has no preferred labeling.
2. **Non-degeneracy:** By the Lemma of §III, every assignment scored is a genuinely distinct set of sentences, and within each set no sentence is scored twice. The peak is a peak over real alternatives.
3. **Structural Isomorphism:** Twelve independent relational constraints satisfied simultaneously by one assignment out of the valid options proves that the domain and the $K_4$ algebra share a structural skeleton.
4. **Repeatable Auditability:** Any party — including an adversarial one — can run the protocol. The audit is a physical measurement of semantic alignment.
5. **Appropriate Truth-Condition:** For claims of the form "Domain A instantiates $K_4$ topology," covariant coherence across the 12 equations is the exact mathematical footprint of structural identity.
6. **Typed Response:** The sweep's output is the 12-bit edge register — six edges × two bits, four states each ($\emptyset$, →, ←, earned ↔) — not a scalar. The measurement is the state per directed edge; $\rho$ is a report drawn from the register, kept for cross-assignment order. Verdicts that cannot name a directed edge were never in the measurement — which is why an under-resolved run now fails *visibly*, as an unwireable register (K7), rather than silently, as a flat curve.

## VI. Audit of Boundaries & Strains

To maintain strict claim discipline, three operational limits are explicitly stated:

### Strain 1 — Evaluator Contamination (Pre-Training Leak)
Synthetic evaluators (LLMs) carry pre-training exposure to classical Western four-fold models (Jung, Aristotle, humors). A cold-state model scoring a Jungian mapping high may be reading the $K_4$ structure or retrieving pre-training text.
* *Mitigation:* Test on **novel, post-training domains** (e.g., modern software APIs, newly engineered materials, obscure technical standards) where pre-training text does not exist.
* *Enforcement:* Step 5 requires human domain-natives from non-Western lineages to eliminate training-data artifacts.

### Strain 2 — Intersubjective Semantic Judgment
The verdict function $V(e_k[\sigma]) \in \{+1, 0, -1\}$ involves semantic comprehension. Two domain-natives may debate whether a specific equation matches a subtle domain relation. The protocol manages this through multi-evaluator panels and noise-floor margins (R2), but it delivers **strong intersubjective verification**, not a 1D laboratory scalar reading.

### The Uniqueness Boundary
Resonance testing verifies that a $K_4$ topology **is present** in the domain. It **does not and cannot prove that $K_4$ is the ONLY structure present**, nor that no other algebra could achieve a resonant peak. Claiming framework uniqueness from a resonance test is a scope error. Uniqueness is established deductively by the dual-binary seed (`ProofI`) and combinatorial closure (`L3-FormalFoundations`), not by resonance sweeps.

## VII. Kill Conditions

* **K1 — The Flat Profile:** If a sweep on a mapped domain yields a flat response curve ($\rho(\sigma) \approx \rho(\sigma')$ for all $\sigma'$), *and Step 1.5 has passed*, the mapping is un-aligned and is struck from the corpus.
* **K2 — The Traveling Peak:** If different independent evaluator lineages peak at different transpositions of the same domain, the peak is an artifact of evaluator bias, and the mapping is falsified.
* **K3 — The Language Collapse:** If a mapping's resonance peak dissolves when evaluated by human domain-natives in non-Indo-European languages (Step 5), the mapping is an artifact of English linguistic packaging and is struck.
* **K4 — The Novel-Domain Null:** If mappings onto novel, post-training 4-element domains systematically fail to produce an R1 peak under LLM evaluation while historical domains succeed, synthetic evaluation is proven to be pure pre-training echo (FM-1).
* **K5 — The Flawless Score:** If a mapping returns $\rho = +1.0$ (12/12) across all evaluators and paraphrases without a single structural tension or wolf-tone, the run is flagged as an evaluator sycophancy failure (FM-4) and rejected.
* **K6 — The Absolute Floor (Swamp Test):** If the canonical assignment produces an R1 peak over competitors but fails the absolute floor ($\rho_V(\sigma) < +0.5$), the mapping is rejected. The least-wrong arrangement of a non-existent structure is an artifact, not a finding.
* **K7 — The Under-Resolved Substitution:** If a flat profile is produced by sentences that fail Step 1.5, the run is void and **the mapping is not struck**. The instrument was measuring nothing. K7 exists because a K1 verdict delivered on under-resolved sentences retires a domain for a failure of the substitution, and that error is silent: a sentence naming no poles comes back unchanged from every assignment and looks exactly like a flat response.
