## I. The Illusion of the Dimensional Descent

When classical analysis sets out to understand a system, it performs a dimensional descent. It starts with the 3D volume, the whole. It slices that into 2D faces, the boundaries. It breaks the faces into 1D edges, the connections. And it reduces the edges to 0D vertices, the fundamental points.

In classical epistemology this is a descent into dead matter. By the time you reach the 0D vertex you have stripped away all interiority, all freedom, all capacity for `.behold()`, and you expect to find a universe built from inert points joined by flat, one-dimensional wires.

That expectation rests on a slide between two different zeroes. *0D* counts extent: a vertex occupies no volume in the ambient space. *0-DoF* counts buffer: a system holding no uncommitted potential, $Q = 0$ (`L3-CyberneticDoFSpectrum` §II.1; expect there: degrees of freedom as the dimension of the decoupled buffer). Classical descent treats the first zero as evidence for the second, and lets the numeral do the arguing. The numeral carries no argument. A vertex has no extent and a full buffer, and the sections below establish that positively.

The Algebra of Four-Fold Distinction forces the descent to reverse. Audit the complete graph on four vertices — the $K_4$ tetrahedron — and you find exactly fifteen sub-structures. As you descend through them, the structure does not flatten. It blossoms back into volume, and what that does to the navigation of any bounded system is the subject of everything that follows.

## V. The Vertex Is Produced From Across: the Secretaries

Every pole is the home of exactly three of the twelve gates — the three equations that solve for it. Call them its **secretaries**: they are the algebra's three ways of producing that pole. The question this section answers is *where they sit*. The answer is the reason a vertex boils.

### V.1 The theorem

> **For every pole $v$, its three secretaries sit on the three edges of the face that excludes $v$ — one secretary per edge, each contributed by a different one of the three faces that contain $v$. Walked in order, they close into a circuit around that excluded face, running against the face's own circulation. On each of those edges the secretary is paired with the excluded face's own reading in the opposite direction, which contains no $v$ at all.**

Said plainly: a vertex is produced entirely from the perimeter of the face it is absent from. None of the vertex sits on that face. All three of the face's edges project it.

### V.2 The derivation

Three facts from the owner of the twelve gates carry it (`L3-EdgeStateSpace`; expect there: §I-d, an equation lives on the face of its AbsentVar; §I-e Step 1, the coherent circulation of the four faces, every shared edge traversed in opposite directions by its two faces; §I-e Step 2, a directed edge is read on the face whose circulation it completes, solved for the third vertex).

1. **A secretary of $v$ lives on a face that contains $v$.** It solves a face relation for $v$, so $v$ is one of that face's three poles. Three faces contain $v$; label each by the pole $w$ it omits.
2. **Its edge is the one opposite $v$ on that face.** By the directed reading rule, a gate is homed at the third vertex of the edge it reads. If the home is $v$, the edge is the face's edge that does not touch $v$: the pair of poles that are neither $v$ nor $w$.
3. **That edge belongs to the face excluding $v$.** It does not touch $v$, so it lies on the triangle of the other three poles — the $v$-excluded face. As $w$ runs over the three poles other than $v$, the edge runs over the three edges of that triangle, one each. Three secretaries, three edges, a bijection.
4. **The direction runs against the grain.** Each secretary reads its edge in the direction that completes its own face's circulation. The $v$-excluded face shares that edge and, by $\partial(\partial\Delta^3) = 0$, traverses it the other way. So all three secretaries walk the excluded face backward, and they close into one circuit. This recovers, from the seating alone, the season identity of `L3-EdgeStateSpace` §I-e Step 5 (expect there: each season's three gates close into a circuit on the triangle of the three non-home poles, the reverse of its home-opposite face's circulation).
5. **The partner on each edge is $v$-free.** The opposite direction on that edge is read by the $v$-excluded face, solved for that face's third vertex — the pole $w$ that the secretary's own face had omitted. The excluded face has $v$ as its AbsentVar, so the partner contains no $v$ anywhere.

Two corollaries fall out at once.

**A pole never presides on an edge it is an end of.** Every secretary of $v$ sits on an edge that does not touch $v$. On its own three edges a pole appears only as material — an operand in other poles' equations, never the one solved for.

**Every edge is the meeting of the two poles it does not touch.** An edge's two gates are the secretaries of the two poles of its complementary edge, facing each other across it in opposite directions. Edge I–R carries SCO, $U$'s secretary, and TAU, $P$'s; its complement is P–U. This is the Law of Intuition seen from the poles' side: the two formulas of an edge solve for the other two poles because each of those poles has seated a secretary there.

#### V.8.e Why the centroid stays: the one unbound point

#### V.8.f The recursion confusion
