## The claim in one sentence

**This is a claim about what physics can see, not about how it writes.**

That distinction is the whole work, and everything else here is an attempt to make it checkable.

A better notation is a preference. Geometric algebra has argued for sixty years that the vector calculus taught since the 1890s was a wrong turn, and mainstream physics shrugged — because the same predictions came out either way. A notational argument that changes nothing observable has earned the shrug.

This is not that argument.

## The amputation

Hamilton's quaternion product is one operation:

$$ab = -(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}) + \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$$

A part that does not care which vector you name first, and a part that flips sign if you swap them. One product, two halves, inseparable.

Gibbs and Heaviside cut it in half. They took the scalar part and the vector part, declared them unrelated operations, and handed them out as separate tools. The convenience was enormous and it won on pedagogy; Tait lost the argument and the quaternions went into the drawer.

What went into the drawer with them was not a notation. It was the fact that those two halves are *the two halves of a boundary crossing*.

The symmetric part is what survives a projection. The antisymmetric part is what a projection deletes. When the two are written as one product that visibly decomposes, that fact is on the page. When they are split into a dot product and a cross product, the discarded half is promoted to an object in its own right — a pseudovector, a thing among things — and its actual character, *the part that cannot cross a boundary*, becomes invisible.

Physics did not lose the ability to calculate. It lost the ability to see that its formalism was continuously performing a subtraction, and what was being subtracted.

## What a boundary subtracts

Once the halves are visible again, the rest follows quickly and in plain terms.

**Orientation is what cannot be sent.** The antisymmetric part of a relation exists only relative to which-one-first. Swap the pair and it changes sign — it was never a property of either one alone. A projection that discards the comparison discards it, necessarily, with no remedy available.

**An interior is not sealed because it is hidden.** It is sealed because it is oriented. Nothing is behind anything. The channel has no way to carry which-way-round and never did. The seal is a subtraction, not a wall.

**A boundary is therefore an operation, not a place.** Anything that discards orientation is a boundary. A measurement does it. A commit to a ledger does it. A published sentence does it. This is why boundaries recur at every scale: symmetrization recurs.

**The same subtraction removes magnitude.** A magnitude in a laboratory unit exists only relative to whose-unit — the thing compared against an apparatus that sits on the exterior side. Like orientation, it is an index of the comparison rather than content of the compared. So ratios cross a boundary and magnitudes do not, for exactly the same reason handedness does not. This is why the framework states relations and refuses values. It is not caution. It is the only thing a projection carries.

## The object

The formal work studies one thing: the complete graph on four vertices, carrying complex impedance, energized under alternating current.

The reason is minimum structure. Bounded interiority — being an inside distinguished from an outside — has a smallest form that supports it. A point carries no relation. A dyad carries one and never closes. A triangle closes and bounds an area, but every point of it is reachable without crossing into anything: surface, no inside. The 3-simplex is the first structure that bounds a volume.

And $K_4$ is the largest complete graph that embeds in the plane. $K_5$ is where planarity fails, by Kuratowski. The object sits exactly on the line between what can be drawn flat and what cannot — which for a study of interiors and exteriors is a theorem, not an image.

Two consequences the corpus takes seriously and states as claims rather than readings. **Time is local.** There is no global foliation — this is ordinary general relativity, and the framework's contribution is only to note that the habit of saying *spacetime* as one word buries it. And **the Minkowski minus sign is algebraic rather than primitive**: biquaternions are $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, the Lorentz group is $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$, and indefinite signature falls out of the star-norm instead of being postulated.

## Why the work looks the way it does

**Its weight is in the configuration, not in any one claim.**

This is the structural fact a reader most often misses, and it is worth stating without metaphor first: roughly ninety-nine percent of a proton's mass is not the rest mass of its constituents. It is the energy of the configuration holding itself together. The mass is in the binding, not in the parts.

The corpus is built the same way, and deliberately. Individual results are frequently ordinary — a shell-capacity sum any chemist knows, a colour-anticolour decomposition from any QFT course, twelve relations from freshman circuit theory. **That is expected and it is not a weakness.** What is claimed is that these seat together in one small topology under mutual constraint, and that moving any one of them meets resistance from the others.

So the test of this work is not whether a constituent is novel. It is whether the joints hold. A reader looking for a startling brick will find none and will have learned nothing about the building.

There is a second reason the ordinariness is expected, and it is the same argument again: **what survives a projection is supposed to look ordinary.** A result that crossed into a domain's vocabulary intact will read as that domain's furniture. Ordinariness is the signature of having crossed.

## The cross-section

Here the analogy does not let the work off, and the corpus does not want it to.

An atom's mass is internal — but the atom is not sealed. It scatters. It bonds. It absorbs. If it had no external coupling you could not weigh it, and from outside, high binding energy and coherent delusion are the same measurement: none.

So the configuration argument *requires* the coupling points. That is what the falsifiers are. They are not rigour-housekeeping and not a gesture toward good practice. They are the scattering cross-section — the only thing that converts internal coherence into a quantity anyone outside can measure.

- **Casimir null-gravity** (`QED-CCP`) — if uncompiled zero-point energy gravitates before collapse, the projection architecture falls.
- **Eötvös departure** (`QED-EotvosBound`) — a stated bound with a stated sign.
- **Syntactic** (`ProofQ_AlgebraicSyntax`) — feed the constraint engine an impossibility; it crashes or the claim is empty.
- **$S_4$ resonance** (`ProofS_ResonantNote`) — permute the pole assignment on a mapped domain. If coherence does not degrade, the mapping was projection and is struck.

The last of these is the one the corpus most owes. Permutation is an exchange operation, so it acts on exactly the oriented content that distinguishes structure from preference — it is the one available instrument that reads the half a projection burns. Mappings published without a sweep have been shipped symmetric: legible, coherent, and missing the property that separates structure from a reading. The debt is carried openly in `llm-reviewer.md` §5 and `internal-todos.md`.

## What cannot be claimed, and why that is the point

We cannot prove an outside. Nothing can — not this framework, not the atom, not the universe. From inside a bounded frame there is no operation that reaches past the boundary and returns with a report.

What *can* be shown is the border.

That is a far stronger position than it sounds, and it is not skepticism. Skepticism says we cannot know whether there is an outside and stops. This says the boundary itself has measurable structure: the tangent's error band widens at a known rate, the divergence sits at a locatable angle, the irreducible error floor has a value. You can characterize a wall in complete detail while claiming nothing whatever about the far side.

**Infinitesimals only take us so far**, and precisely: a limit presumes the coordinate survives the approach — that you can keep halving through the region of interest. At a tangent singularity the coordinate is what fails. So the limit process is the one operation that cannot report on the place it approaches, and an irreducible $\epsilon$ is the statement that there is a floor under the halving. The method stops not from insufficient care but because it assumes the continuity the boundary denies.

Wittgenstein reached silence and left it featureless — one wall, nothing further to say. This work keeps the silence entire and gives it a diagnosis: the same silence, read as a turn rather than a stop (`Meta-Singularity-and-Image`). Nothing crosses. The character of the boundary is what is recovered, and it is the only thing available from inside.

## What the corpus asks of a reader

Not agreement. Three things:

**Check something small first.** `llm.md` opens with three checks that take five minutes by hand. If one fails, say which and stop — that is a finding and it is worth more than assent.

**Test a joint, not a brick.** Find two claims that should resist each other and see whether they do. A loose joint is a real result.

**Run a permutation sweep in your own field** (`ProofS_ResonantNote`). Reassign the poles and report whether coherence degrades. A flat response is a kill, and publishing one would be the most useful thing anyone outside this corpus could do for it.

The open problems are kept in public — `internal-todos.md` lists derivations not yet performed, stated in the negative; `llm-reviewer.md` §5 lists known unpaid debts, including a trials-factor objection filed against one of the corpus's own results before any reviewer raised it.

It provides a base to work *from*.

Test the algebra in and against your own domain (`ProofS`). Examine the executable models in `/code/`. Find the limits of the topology — then recurse and expand into the volume.
