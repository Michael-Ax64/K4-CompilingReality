# The Six Walls
## A Filing Tool for Every Way a Finite Exterior Fails an Interior

*L3, instrumental. A pondering tool, not a derivation. Companion to `L3-SoficityStar.md`, which keeps the definitions, and to `L3-NonSoficTopologicalVolumes.md`, which keeps the existence result. This file keeps the sorting.*

---

## 0. What This Is

`L3-SoficityStar.md` names two walls, approximation and readout, and places them on the two edges of the Mutable plane. That file also raised a worse version of this question — six named carriers against six edges — and retired it, correctly, because the carrier list is a census and a census cannot be matched against a forced count.

This file asks the question the right way round, and the difference is the whole method. **Nothing is matched.** Each category names *which two of the four ingredients it is about*, and the edge is whatever that pair happens to be. Categories can collide onto one edge. They can leave edges empty. Either outcome is informative, and the method is worth running only because both were available.

Both happened. The occupancy that came back is lopsided and one edge is bare, which is reported in §IV rather than tidied away — and an earlier draft of this file did tidy it away, by manufacturing a category so that the count came out even. That draft is corrected in place and the correction is kept visible, because a filing tool that reports full occupancy has usually been fitted to report it.

The tool is **open at the item level and closed at the category level.** The list of discoveries will never be complete. The list of things they are *about* can be.

---

## I. The List That Will Not Close

Thirteen entries as the author's catalog currently stands, from fields that do not cite each other. Each found a way that a finite outside fails to reach a real inside. The trailing comma is load-bearing.

Weyl and Aharonov–Bohm and Wu–Yang and Berry on non-integrable phase, Hopf and Dirac on the absent global section, Laue and Noether and ADM on quasi-local energy, Gauss and Buividovich–Polikarpov and Donnelly–Wall and Donnelly–Freidel on edge modes, Gromov and Weiss and the 2026 construction on group non-soficity, Connes through MIP\* = RE on the operator algebras, 't Hooft and Nielsen–Ninomiya on anomalies, Markov and Higman on loop topology from dimension four, Voiculescu and Bowen and Airey–Bowen on model-dependent entropy, Geroch–Hartle on undecidability where the walls meet, Geroch and Bernal–Sánchez on causality as the guarantee, Callan–Harvey and Kaplan and Freed–Hopkins on the five-dimensional repair, the interpretability literature on the same failure in silicon,

The list grows. Treating its length as a quantity was the error `L3-SoficityStar` §VIII retired. Treating its *content* as sortable is a different operation, and it is the one available.

## II. From Items to Categories

Group the list by what each item is about, and the categories come out. The grouping is done before any pole is mentioned, which matters: if the poles were in hand while grouping, the grouping would be the tool leaving a mark (FM-12).

| Category | What it says a finite exterior cannot do | Items |
|---|---|---|
| **Approximability** | Build the structure out of finite pieces | group non-soficity; the operator algebras; anomalies; loop topology from dimension four |
| **Non-integrability** | Find the content at a point | Weyl, Aharonov–Bohm, Wu–Yang, Berry; and interpretability |
| **No global section** | Make one consistent choice everywhere | Hopf, Dirac |
| **Quasi-locality** | Read a local density; only a boundary total crosses | Laue, Noether, ADM |
| **Non-factorizability** | Cut without creating something | Gauss's law; Buividovich–Polikarpov; Donnelly–Wall; Donnelly–Freidel |

**Five, and an earlier draft of this file said six.** It carried *Decidability* as a sixth peer, taking Markov, Higman and Geroch–Hartle. That was the tool leaving a mark after all, and the catalog says where it actually belongs: Markov and Higman are a model-class failure and file under approximability, while the undecidability result is **interface-generic** — it holds wherever two of the walls meet, on every cut, which is a statement about the facing structure rather than about any one contrast. It is plane-tier, not an edge category. Retired here; see §IV.

Four of the thirteen entries therefore sit above the edge tier: model-dependent entropy and generic undecidability on the planes, the causal guarantee and the five-dimensional repair at the centroid. Nine are edge items.

Five categories, from a list that has no final length. Add a discovery tomorrow and it files into one of these or it breaks the set, and §VIII says what breaking it would mean.

## III. The Four Ingredients, and Why Their Poles Are Not Chosen

`L3-SoficityStar` §II strips the soficity scheme to four ingredients: the **local rules** that fix the structure, the **class of finite models** the exterior may build, the **normalized defect** measured between them, and the **verdict**, floor or no floor.

Read each on the dual-binary seed, whose charges are fixed at `About-Methodology` §I and derived at `L3-EdgeStateSpace` §XI: $P$ is $(+\,+)$ Active and Asserting, $U$ is $(+\,-)$ Active and Yielding, $R$ is $(-\,+)$ Reactive and Asserting, $I$ is $(-\,-)$ Reactive and Yielding. The Route Law is consistent with the second binary independently: the four squares in the twelve home at $P$ and $R$, the two roots at $U$ and $I$.

- The **verdict** is a committed scalar the test emits. Emitted, so Active; committed, so Asserting.
- The **local rules** are the structure's own articulation — what it says of itself. Emitted by the structure, so Active; articulation yields rather than commits, so Yielding.
- The **normalized defect** is a measurement taken between rules and model, responsive to whichever model is supplied. Received, so Reactive; a ratio, which the Route Law makes Yielding.
- The **finite models** are the substrate the exterior supplies. Received by the test, so Reactive; built and committed, so Asserting.

*An earlier draft of this file had the Active/Reactive axis inverted for the middle two bullets, reading the defect as Active and the rules as Reactive. The landing was unaffected — the survivor below is the same assignment and the fall-off is unchanged — but two of the four reasons were wrong, and `About-Methodology` §I is the citation of record that settles them.*

Four ingredients, four distinct coordinate pairs. So the assignment to poles is not a choice; it is read off.

### The sweep

Score every one of the $4! = 24$ assignments by how many of the eight binary coordinates it gets right.

```
score 8/8 :  1 assignment     verdict->P   defect->I   rules->U   models->R
score 6/8 :  4 assignments
score 4/8 : 14 assignments
score 2/8 :  4 assignments
score 0/8 :  1 assignment
```

One survives. The fall-off is monotone and steep, which is what `ProofS_ResonantNote` asks of a sort: coherence must degrade under the alternatives, or the fit belongs to the evaluator rather than the structure.

**One correction this file owes.** `L3-SoficityStar` §VIII says a permutation sweep is vacuous for a four-term sort, because $\mathrm{Aut}(K_4) = S_4$ acts freely on assignments of four terms to four vertices and leaves a single orbit. That is true of *bare* vertices and it is why the sweep above is not a graph-automorphism sweep. The poles here are not bare. They carry the dual-binary seed, and the seed is exactly the symmetry-breaking that gives the 24 assignments something to be measured against. The sweep is live because the vertices mean something before the test begins.

**What the sweep does not establish.** The eight binary readings in the four bullets above are judgments, stated in the open so they can be disputed one at a time. The sweep shows that *given those readings*, one assignment survives. It does not show the readings are right. Dispute any single bullet and the surviving assignment may move.

## IV. The Six Edges, as Consequence

Now each category names two ingredients. The edge is whatever pair that is. Nothing is placed.

| Category | The two ingredients it is about | Edge | Plane | Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Approximability | rules against models — can the built ground honour the stated rules? | $U$–$R$ | Mutable | **4** |
| Quasi-locality | verdict against defect — does the scalar that crosses carry the flow inside? | $P$–$I$ | Mutable | 1 |
| Non-factorizability | defect against models — does cutting the substrate create flow of its own? | $I$–$R$ | Fixed | 1 |
| Non-integrability | defect against rules — is the circulation expressible as stated point values? | $I$–$U$ | Cardinal | 2 |
| No global section | verdict against models — can one committed choice cover the whole ground? | $P$–$R$ | Cardinal | 1 |
| — | verdict against rules | $P$–$U$ | Fixed | **0** |

**Five edges occupied, one empty, and the occupancy is lopsided.** An earlier draft of this file reported six distinct edges with no collisions and no empties, and called that outcome *free to fail and didn't*. Run against the author's catalog it did fail, and the failure is the finding. Four items land on $U$–$R$ together; $P$–$U$ takes none.

The six-for-six had been obtained by splitting approximability into *approximability and decidability*, which manufactured a category to occupy an edge — exactly the move §0 claims the method avoids, committed one level up. The honest reading is the asymmetry:

> **Approximation is one edge with a ladder running along it. Readout is four edges.**

And the reason is structural rather than accidental. Readout failures differ in *what* fails to cross — loop content, a section, a density, a factorization — so they spread. Approximation failures all say the identical thing and differ only in *which model class* fails: finite permutations, matricial algebras, finite spectra, finite presentations. Model class is the ladder (`L3-SoficityStar` §III), and a ladder runs **along** an edge rather than across several. The four items stack without colliding because the edge's interior is graded.

The empty edge earns its keep too. $P$–$U$ is verdict against rules — *does an answer follow from the presentation* — which is where undecidability would sit if undecidability were a wall. The catalog places it above the edge tier instead, as what appears wherever two walls meet, on every cut. So the edge is empty of primary failures and its subject matter lives one tier up. §VIII records what that vacancy is and is not.

The two Mutable entries are the two walls `L3-SoficityStar` §VIII already placed there, arrived at here from the other direction. That file placed them by reading the walls; this one places them by reading the categories, and they land on the same two edges. An independent second route to a standing CANDIDATE is the cheapest evidence available, and it is worth exactly what it costs.

*One naming discrepancy, flagged rather than resolved. The catalog and this table put Laue–Noether–ADM under quasi-locality at $P$–$I$ and the edge modes under non-factorizability at $I$–$R$. The treatise's Table 1 has those two at swapped edges, reading Laue–Noether–ADM as the whole that will not factor and the cut-as-third-party as the quasi-locality occupant. Both readings are defensible and the occupancy count is unaffected either way, since both edges are occupied under both. The discrepancy wants settling before either document is cited for the other's assignment.*

## V. The Three Planes

The planes were not consulted. Two of the three came out sorted anyway; the third does not survive the occupancy correction, and saying so is the point of keeping a record.

**Cardinal — $\{P\text{–}R,\ I\text{–}U\}$ — the fibre plane. Stands.** No global section and non-integrability. A section is a fibre value chosen over every base point. A holonomy is fibre content accumulated around a base loop. Both are one statement: *the fibre is not determined by the base.* Both bundle failures land in one plane, and no other failure does. Both edges occupied.

**Mutable — $\{P\text{–}I,\ U\text{–}R\}$ — the two primary walls. Stands.** Approximability and quasi-locality. Can the interior be built from finite parts, and can it be read from outside. Construction and observation, which `L3-SoficityStar` §V calls the approximation wall and the readout wall. Both edges occupied, one of them four deep.

**Fixed — $\{P\text{–}U,\ I\text{–}R\}$ — the separation plane. Collapses.** The reading was that decidability asks whether a procedure separates yes-instances from no-instances while factorizability asks whether a cut separates inside from outside, so that both are *can the exterior draw a clean line?* The reading was good and its occupant was invented. With decidability retired to the plane tier, $P$–$U$ is empty and this plane holds one occupied edge and one vacancy. What remains true is the vacancy's own structure (§VIII), not a two-edge subject.

So: two planes with a shared subject and two occupied edges each; one plane half-empty, with its missing half being the subject that turned out to live a tier higher. That is a weaker result than the earlier draft claimed and a more informative one, because it says where the tiers actually divide.

The corpus's three modalities are supposed to be exactly three irreducible ways a four-fold structure can be bisected. Two of them here come out as irreducible ways an exterior can fail. That is a correspondence over two cases rather than three, and `ProofS` would want the category-to-pair assignments swept before it is more than that.

*A second and independent plane sort exists and has not been reconciled with this one. Collecting the twelve by coefficient vector shows them to be four relations solved three ways each, those four relations being the four faces of the tetrahedron, so that each edge is the meeting of two faces. Sorting the edges by whether their two faces are of the same type puts Cardinal alone on one side and Fixed and Mutable together on the other — a different cut from the one above. Whether the two sorts are the same fact is open.*

## VI. The One Item Whose Resolution Is Not on the Surface

The anomaly files under approximability, at $U$–$R$: no finite lattice realizes the symmetry site by site. But it is the only item in the list whose *resolution* sits nowhere on the tetrahedron's edges.

Callan–Harvey (1985) and Kaplan (1992) resolve it by making the four-dimensional theory the boundary of a five-dimensional bulk. Freed and Hopkins (2016) classified what that bulk is: an invertible topological field theory, which has no local degrees of freedom, no metric dependence, no particles — only a global phase. That is the centroid as the corpus already describes it: five-dimensional, integration rather than extension, seating $\omega$ and actuating nothing (`L3-TheAtomOfSpace`, the $\omega$-Law).

So the anomaly is the item that shows the tool's shape. It fails on an edge and it is repaired at the centre, and the centre is not a seventh slot. It is the condition under which the other four are held together, which `L3-SoficityStar` §VIII already assigns to the quantifier *for every finite piece*.

## VII. Using It

Filing a new discovery takes two questions and no judgment about where it ought to go.

1. **What can a finite exterior not do here?** The answer names one of the five categories, or it names none.
2. **Which two ingredients is that about?** Rules, models, defect, verdict. The pair gives the edge.

Some test cases, including the ones that behave badly.

- **Gödel incompleteness.** No answer follows from the presentation. Decidability, $P$–$U$. Files cleanly, and puts Gödel on the same edge as Markov and Geroch–Hartle, which is a reading the corpus has wanted and can now state at an address.
- **Arrow's impossibility.** No aggregation rule realizes the stated conditions. Approximability, $U$–$R$. A social-choice theorem files on the same edge as Connes, which is either the tool working or the tool being too loose, and that is a real question about it.
- **Black-hole information.** A boundary total that does not carry the interior. Quasi-locality, $P$–$I$.
- **MIP\* = RE.** Straddles two edges. It is an *undecidability* result ($P$–$U$) about an *approximability* question ($U$–$R$), and the two edges share the $U$ vertex. The tool reports the straddle rather than forcing a choice, which is the behaviour wanted.
- **Heisenberg uncertainty.** Conjugate observables have no joint value at a point, which reads as non-integrability, $I$–$U$. The fit is poor: uncertainty is a structural feature of conjugate pairs, not a finite exterior failing to reach something. **Flagged as a bad fit rather than filed.**
- **The measurement problem.** Files nowhere, and should not. It is not a statement that an exterior fails to reach an interior. Out of scope, which is different from being a seventh category.

## VIII. What Would Break It

Three kills, in order of how much they would cost.

**A discovery that fits no category and is in scope.** This is the one that matters. It would mean the category set is not closed, and the tool's whole claim is that the items are open and the categories are not. Note what this kill is *not*: a new category landing on an already-occupied edge would be ordinary, since §IV already carries four items on one edge and the collision would just be another rung on that ladder. The kill is a failure to file at all — an in-scope obstruction that names no pair of the four ingredients, which would mean the four-ingredient scheme itself is short a term.

**A second ingredient-to-pole assignment scoring 8/8 on different binary readings.** The sweep's authority rests on the four readings in §III being the natural ones. A competing set of readings that is equally natural and yields a different survivor would show the sort is the evaluator's.

**A category whose two ingredients are not two.** Every entry in §IV names exactly two. A category that is irreducibly about three ingredients would belong to a face rather than an edge, and the tool would be a different shape — informative, but not this one.

## IX. Claim Sort

**LANDED (external).** Every item in §I at its own address. The anomaly's five-dimensional resolution and the invertible classification of the bulk (§VI).

**LANDED (this file).** The sweep's arithmetic: given the four binary readings of §III, one assignment of 24 scores 8/8, with the stated fall-off. The arithmetic is mechanical; the readings it runs on are not.

**OPEN VOTE.** The five categories as a closed set (§II). The four binary readings (§III). The category-to-ingredient-pair assignments (§IV), including the Laue-versus-edge-modes naming discrepancy against the treatise's Table 1.

**CANDIDATE.** The three-plane sort of §V, as a second route to `L3-SoficityStar` §VIII's two-wall placement and as a new reading of the Cardinal and Fixed planes. Kill: a category-to-pair assignment, equally natural, that scatters the pairs across planes without loss of coherence.

**NOT CLAIMED.** That the occupancy pattern of §IV is evidence of anything beyond the current catalog. That any item in §I was derived here. That the tool predicts a discovery not yet made.

**RETIRED.** *Decidability* as a sixth peer category (§II) — it is plane-tier, interface-generic, and was manufactured to even out a count. The one-to-one edge assignment an earlier draft reported (§IV) — it does not hold against the author's catalog. The Fixed plane as the separation plane (§V) — the reading was sound and its second occupant was the invented category.

**Register note.** This is a pondering instrument. Its value is that it files a growing list into a fixed shape and makes three things visible that a list cannot show: which failures share a plane, which items straddle two edges, and which candidates do not fit. None of that is a derivation, and the file should not be cited as one.
