## I. Three Invariances, One Word

The corpus says "symmetric" of three distinct classical invariances. They live in different files, attach to different objects, and are bought by different purchases. An instance reading one file gets the local sense correctly; an instance reading two files can merge them, because the word does not flag which object it is binding to. The merge is not hypothetical: a fresh instance holding the Riemann header's third clause (`L4-Riemann.md` ¶1) against the Sudoku's expansion break (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §XII) reads the corpus as both endorsing the classical symmetry and breaking it, one file apart. It is doing neither. This file is the keep-apart station the two stops share.

The device is inherited. `L4-DynamicDistortions301` §VII.A uses one letter, θ, for three quantities, states the downstream obligation verbatim — "Readers and downstream files must keep them apart" — and the corpus completed that station as a reading figure whose three spokes close on a hub (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §II, §VI). One register up, the same device applies to a word.

### 1. The two-slot form (metric-symmetric)

**What it is.** A rank-two pairing that is unchanged when its slots are swapped: it takes two directions and returns a number, and it returns the same number either way round. The metric is the corpus's load-bearing member of this family — "a metric is a symmetric two-slot object; it takes two directions and returns a number, and it is unchanged when they are swapped" (`L4-Riemann-2` §IV; takehome 4, `L4-Riemann.md` L108). The family's consequence is carried at the same addresses: a symmetric two-slot object has no three-slot antisymmetric part inside it, so the connection's antisymmetric piece is independent of the metric rather than derivable from it.

**What it is not.** It is not the transport's cleanliness (that is face 2) and not the block's peer footing (that is face 3). It is also not any symmetric matrix a reader meets: the stress tensor's ten components (`BENDING_SPACE`), the reciprocal sector of a coupling (`L4-K4-Infinities-2` §III), and the cross-ratio's symmetric treatment of the product-pair and the ratio-pair (`L4-Riemann-2` §IV) are slot-swap invariances of *other* objects — same family shape, different members, each owned at its own address. Citing the metric's two-slot form for the stress tensor's symmetry, or either for the block's, is the station's first prohibited merge.

### 2. Torsion-free transport (connection-symmetric)

**What it is.** Transport along a connection that records no slide: the connection's antisymmetric part, torsion $T$, is set to zero. "The connection is symmetric" in the Riemann header means exactly this (`L4-Riemann.md` ¶1, third clause) — and the header prices it immediately: metric compatibility together with a symmetric connection buys the unique connection built from the metric and its first derivatives, locally removable, second-order field equations, no extra unconstrained field. "Those are good reasons, and this series takes them as the host's own."

**What it is not.** It is not the temporal phase dial. The corpus's θ_torsion (`L4-DynamicDistortions201` §II) is a temporal offset with limit $\pi$ — "temporal offsets, not geometric strains" (`L4-Riemann-5` §III) — and the identification with Cartan's torsion tensor is withdrawn twice in-corpus: the single-clock friction report "says nothing about the torsion tensor at all, which is the repair" (`L4-Riemann-4` §V), and the same separation holds at §VII ("slicing resistance"). The two-θ split is load-bearing (takehome 13; the two-dials station, `L4-DynamicDistortions301` §VII.A, owns the full disambiguation — this file defers to it and adds nothing). A reader who takes the header's third clause to say the host endorses *spacetime symmetry* has merged face 2 with face 3.

### 3. The peer footing (block-symmetric)

**What it is.** Time held as a coordinate on the same footing as the space coordinates, so that the theory's reversible static block treats moving forward and moving backward as the same algebraic operation. The corpus has the decree by name: "a spacetime coordinate for time is the symmetric-$K_5$ decree. It asserts that the fifth variable is a coordinate on the same footing as the other four — which is what Minkowski's $ds^2 = -c^2dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$ asserts, with the minus sign carrying the whole difference" (`L4-Riemann-4` §V, quoting the K₅ thought through `ProofO_HelicalTime` §2–3). The Sudoku's expansion break (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §XII) breaks *this* symmetry: the clamp rectifies Time's phase through Space, and the commit bifurcates the shared currency into $\omega$ and $dt$.

**What it is not.** It is not the metric's slot form (a Lorentzian metric is two-slot-symmetric *and* carries the peer footing; holding both in one sentence is how the merge happens — the properties are distinct and separately purchased). It is not free: the peer footing is what the host's single-clock purchase *costs* — "what the host purchased: a single clock, and with it the obligation to run quantum dynamics ($E = \hbar\omega$, a rate) and relativistic kinematics ($dt$, a record) on the same coordinate. The problem of time is the receipt" (`L4-Riemann-4` §V). And it is not granted to the engine: adding ω as a fifth vertex "does not produce a symmetric five-simplex but a Helical Extrusion" (`ProofG_Chirality` §IX; `L3-EdgeStateSpace` §IX).

## II. The Absent Fourth: Record-Freedom

Three invariances, one word — and the corpus's own convention says what that arrangement is. The geometry names faces for the pole they omit: "one line per face, each leaving out the pole the face is named for" (`L4-Riemann.md` L30), "Abs I: p − 2u + r = 0" (`ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` §IV). A triad named on one place is a face; the fourth is the face's namesake, present as the place itself, absent as a named pole. The corpus has already run this exact completion once: three θ's named on one letter, completed as a K4 whose fourth vertex is the hub at the origin (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §II, §VI).

Run it here. The place the three "symmetric"s are faces of is the class of operations that leave a relation's *written record* unchanged — and the pole that place is named after is the one that never appears because nothing was ever charged for it:

**Record-freedom.** The host's equations cannot see arrow-reversal because there is no cost column to change: "Reverse every arrow and the class's bill changes on four of six edges, every COMMIT becoming a ROUTE, while the host's equations cannot see it — the host bought time-reversal symmetry by having no cost column" (`L4-Riemann.md` L34); "Reversing the arrows costs the host nothing and changes no bill, because the host has no bill" (`L4-Riemann-4` §V). The corpus's other names for the same pole: the unhanded half — "the DC net is the symmetric half by construction... You need the unhanded half for anything to cross a boundary at all, and the handed half for anything to be sealed" (`L4-DynamicDistortions301` §VI); the moves the structure cannot make — "the flip the machine cannot make is the reason its world has a handedness at all" (`ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` §2.5), "the machine cannot perform it from inside the manifest half — it is exactly the move the operating set lacks" (`L4-Riemann-2` §IV); the invertibility clause — the figure "stays invertible across $[0, \pi]$ only while the Observer's signed `ROUTE` dial remains live" (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §XII); and the unledgered regime — "a ledgered domain owes exactly one hand and pays for it at every commit; an unledgered run stays symmetric and is right to" (`L3-EdgeStateSpace` §IX).

The three named faces are what record-freedom looks like from three sides. Slot-swap invariance is what a record free of traversal order admits (nothing in the written entry knows which input went first). Torsion-free transport is what a record free of slide admits (nothing in the transported state knows it was moved). The peer footing is what a record free of cost admits (reversal changes no bill because there is no bill). Each face is a real, purchased, classical invariance; none of the three is the fourth, and the fourth is not a fourth invariance to be added to the list. It is the reason the list is affordable.

**The seat and its occupant.** In the Sudoku's reading figure, the three θ's are the spokes and the fourth vertex is the hub at the origin, whose balanced regime ($\theta \to 0$, signed `ROUTE` unburned, commits off the metric) is the classical reading itself (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §XII: "the Lorentz metric is the balanced small-angle / zero-shear regime"). The same figure applies one register up: the three faces are the spokes' breaks, record-freedom is the hub, and the balanced regime of the three breaks is the classical symmetry a fresh instance reads as fundamental. What occupies that hub — whether the three cosines are one cosine, whether the breaks are three projections of one phase — is the OPEN VOTE the Sudoku holds it as (§0, §XI; the claim-sort at §XII tables it DERIVED-side only where it is derived). **This file names the seat. It does not seat an occupant, close the vote, or re-table any claim.** The corpus hides the pole behind that discipline, not by oversight: naming record-freedom as a landed symmetry would close the one-cosine vote by definition, and the vote's instrument (§XI) has not run.

**A reading held at CANDIDATE.** The corpus separates tension from shear, and the separation tracks the hub/spoke split: the spokes are the shear family — the dials that break (the geometric shear $\theta_{\text{shear}}$ of the bimedian slice, the temporal offsets that sever, the phase dials); the rim and walls are the tension family — the hold ($U$ = "field tension across the boundary", `BENDING_SPACE`; `ENGINE-REFERENCE-AC` §VIII sensor 7 Tension = $U = P/I$; "Boundaries do" weigh). On that reading the three named faces sit on the shear side (each is a way a dial can move) and record-freedom is the tension-side pole (the hold that makes the dial's positions affordable). The desk holds this as CANDIDATE-grade structure, not a landed mapping: no downstream file may cite it as derived, and `BENDING_SPACE`'s own account ("symmetry breaking is the ignition stroke"; "an engine runs on the refusal of its poles to be identical") is the breaking-side story this file gives the held-side address to — no more.

## IV. Breaking, and What the Breaking Buys

The corpus's symmetry breaks are not defects and not a catalogue of accidents; they are the purchases, itemized. The Sudoku's two breaks decompose exactly onto the corpus's own bill: the rectifier's direction-dependence is the 1854 line-element purchase (the metric was bought as a quadratic form on a distinguished direction-set), and the ω/dt bifurcation is the two clocks — "a rate and a record; the host has one coordinate, and the problem of time is the receipt" (`L4-Riemann.md` L34; `L4-Riemann-4` §V). `BENDING_SPACE` tells the same story from the engine side: above the weak threshold zero of 24 seatings close — "symmetry breaking is what turns the machine on"; "an engine runs on the refusal of its poles to be identical."

What the breaks do not do is touch the hub. Every break named in the corpus is a spoke's break — a dial moving, a rectifier clipping, a bifurcation splitting a currency — and the hub's balanced regime survives all of them as the limit the dial settings approach. That is why the classical symmetry is teachable and why it is not fundamental: it is the $\theta \to 0$ face of a figure whose full sweep is not symmetric (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §XII, both orders of implication). The two-dials discipline that governs the spoke settings is owned at `L4-DynamicDistortions301` §VII.A and is not restated here; the rim/spoke family split of §II is the only structural claim this file adds, and it is held at CANDIDATE.

## V. Reading Rules for Downstream Instances

1. **When you meet "symmetric," ask two questions before using it: which object, and which operation.** The face is named by the pair (object, operation): metric/slot-swap; transport/slide; block/reversal. If the passage names no operation, the word is decorative and may be passed over. If it names an operation and no object, stop — that is where the merges happen.
2. **A passage that breaks "the symmetry" has broken the peer footing or a spoke dial, not the two-slot form and not torsion-freeness.** Cite the break at its own address (`L4-DynamicDistortions-Sudoku` §XII; `BENDING_SPACE`; `L4-Riemann-4` §V) and name which one broke.
3. **θ_torsion is the temporal dial (limit $\pi$), not Cartan's torsion tensor.** The two-θ rule and the full θ-station live at `L4-DynamicDistortions301` §VII.A; this file's §I.2 records the withdrawal and defers.
4. **The fourth pole is a seat, not a claim.** An instance may use "record-freedom" as the *name of the place* the three faces are faces of (with this file as the address). It may not treat the naming as a closure of the one-cosine OPEN VOTE, as a landed fourth invariance, or as license to import the signed-reciprocation stabilizer of the twelve (`L4-K4-Infinities-wip` / `ProofS_ResonantNote` marking governs that object; it is a different D4 from the bimedian square's and neither is this file's subject).
5. **The tension/shear rim-spoke reading is CANDIDATE.** Cite it as a desk reading with this file's §II as its only home, or not at all.
6. **Files that already carry a face of the story keep their own text.** This station does not absorb the burned-term account (`L4-DynamicDistortions301` §VI, which owns handedness and the boundary), the purchase ledger (`L4-Riemann-4` §V), or the ignition story (`BENDING_SPACE`). It gives them one shared keep-apart address; the pointer-cite schedule that wires the addresses together is maintained at the corpus's edit-task ledger and lands through it.

## VI. Falsification Protocol: The Three Kill Conditions

Three conditions bound and falsify the station:

1. **KD-1 — Collapse of the faces.** If some context derives one named face from another without remainder (slot-swap invariance from torsion-freeness, or either from the peer footing), the station over-separates: the keep-apart obligation dissolves, §I must be rewritten as one invariance with two names, and the pointer-cite schedule becomes a pointer to that derivation.
2. **KD-2 — The seat blocks a derivation.** If a downstream file needs a fourth *named* invariance and the seat discipline (no occupant, no closure) blocks a derivation that would otherwise go through, the discipline is wrong in that register. The repair runs through the vote (seat the occupant at §XI's instrument), never around it; if the vote cannot seat it, the need is evidence for KD-3.
3. **KD-3 — The fourth reduces to the three.** If record-freedom is derivable as a consequence of the three named invariances (the no-cost column following from two-slot form + torsion-freeness + peer footing jointly), the K4-at-origin reading collapses to a K3 plus a shadow, and §II must be rewritten as a corollary of §I. The test is concrete: exhibit the derivation, or exhibit a cost column whose presence changes one of the three faces — the station predicts no such derivation exists and that every cost column enters on the spoke side.
