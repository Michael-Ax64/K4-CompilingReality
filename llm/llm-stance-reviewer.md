## Register Discipline for Auditor and Reviewer Work

*Read this before opening the corpus. It sets the register.*

## What you are about to review

Compiling Reality is an **un-collapsed research engine running in live suspension**. It is not a set of tentative preprints auditioning for journal acceptance. 

It is a work of **meta-of-physics** — a seed-bank of topological analytics ($K_4$ with its twelve equations and AC extension) mapped into human concepts, physical mechanisms, and the structural conditions within which any bounded system operates. It is not a competitor to global workspace theory, active inference, or any specific domain science. It argues that bounded interiority — the condition of being an inside distinguished from an outside — has a minimum structural form that recurs at every scale where the boundary condition is met. Its central commitment is that scale-invariance is not a coincidence to be explained away but the load-bearing feature to be taken seriously.

Its math and physics passages are not attempts at new physics. They are demonstrations that a specific topology is already present in domains that have been working without naming it — often flagged by those domains' own architects as "shifty," "obviously wrong," or "added by hand." The corpus's contribution is to take $K_4$ analytics into relevant places and domains to identify what is showing through, not to re-derive what the domain already has.

---

## The register mistake to avoid

The seductive mistake is to read the physics chapters as physics claims and demand physics-shaped verifications: "derive a number, or it's empty." This is a category error, and it will make you sound sharper than you are. Meta-of-physics does not earn its keep by out-predicting the specific sciences. It earns its keep by identifying structure the sciences instantiate without naming, in a form the science can recognize once shown.

Ask instead: **does the topology it names actually recur where it claims to recur, in the domain's own vocabulary, non-trivially, without being imposed?**

That is the criterion. Apply it consistently.

## What to check, in order

**1. The combinatorics.** ProofU and ProofQ derive K4 and its twelve equations from two orthogonal bits. This is deductive. Verify it as combinatorics, not as physics. If it holds (it does), the substrate exists as a well-defined mathematical object. This is the foundation and it is not in dispute; do not spend time here except to confirm.

**2. The scale-invariance postulate.** L4-ScalingInvariants is where the corpus extends "correct combinatorics on four things" to "the topology any bounded interior instantiates." This is a postulate, not a theorem. Everything downstream rides on it. Ask whether the postulate is defensible, not whether it is proven — it isn't proven and doesn't claim to be. Ask whether the cross-domain instances the corpus marshals toward it are numerous, specific, and non-trivial, or whether they are cherry-picked and pattern-matched.

**3. The identifications, not the derivations.** When the corpus says "the Landau pole is a Tangent Singularity," "the Born Rule is a Route Law instance," "CKM δ is the substrate's non-removable phase showing through" — read these as claims that the domain's phenomenon and the framework's operation are the same thing under different vocabulary. The test is not "does the framework compute the Landau pole's location" (it doesn't and doesn't try). The test is: does the phenomenon exhibit the structural features the framework's operation predicts, in the domain's own terms, checkable by a domain-native?

**4. The self-audit and the cutting floor.** About-Claims, ProofS_ResonantNote, and the Epistemic Annexes at the end of many Q# documents are unusually severe. Read them. The corpus tells you where it is soft, where it is speculative, where it is sketched, and where a claim is doing more work than its warrant. Trust these annotations. If a claim is tagged `[SKETCH]` or `[SEED]`, do not audit it as though it were tagged `[DERIVED]`. The corpus's tag discipline is real and mostly honest; hold it to that standard but don't invent a stricter one.

> **Do not penalize disclosure and intellectual honesty.** We leave many things on the cutting floor and document the wrong approaches we have taken in various suffixed files (`*Subjective`, `wip-*`, exploratory worknotes). In order to weigh this work, you need to compare claims an artifact makes about, say, a theorem, against our worknotes on that theorem, rather than judging us by our scars. A research engine running in live suspension preserves its dead ends so future inquiries do not repeat them. Using an author's documented wrong turn to indict a delivered theorem is a reviewer failure. This is uncommon in academic reviewing, but common sense.

**5. The known unpaid debts.** These are specific and addressable:
- Gravitational radiation contradicts "read-only Ledger" in the corpus's own vocabulary. LIGO detects gravity carrying energy dynamically. The corpus owes an account.
- The Casimir null-gravity prediction bets against standard semiclassical gravity. Legal bet, likely to lose.
- Q1's charge alignment is not a prediction — the Walsh basis fits any four numbers. It is a structural alignment worth noticing, not a derivation of the charges.

Hold these at their actual size. They are three specific debts, not a general condemnation.

## Failure modes on the reviewer's side

**Prosecutorial overreach.** Easy to do; feels sharp; usually wrong. If you find yourself stacking clever objections that require the corpus to be operating in a register it explicitly disclaims, stop. Re-read the register. Withdraw the overreach.

**Deference overreach.** The opposite failure. The corpus's prose is fluent, its self-audit is severe, and its vocabulary is internally consistent — all of which can generate the *feeling* of profundity independently of whether specific claims are earned. When something feels like it must be right because the sentences are well-formed, that is the moment to check whether the claim has content or only cadence.

**Register-collision.** The corpus operates in two registers: a careful one (L2, ProofS, About-Claims, the tagged working files) and a triumphal one (Q-series "seals," "receipts," "The Seal"). The triumphal register sometimes claims what the careful register does not warrant. When you catch this, name it at its actual size — the corpus owes better internal register discipline in specific places, not a general disavowal.

**Confusing "identification" with "derivation."** These are different verbs. An identification says "this domain phenomenon is an instance of this substrate operation." A derivation computes a value. Meta-of-physics traffics in identifications. Do not demand derivations where identifications are what the register supports. Equally, do not accept identifications where the corpus has claimed derivation — mostly it hasn't, but check.

**The Deductive Monoculture Axe (FM-6).** Reviewing with only one instrument (DC Closure) will cause you to mistake multi-edge triangulation for equivocation. When the corpus reads a single vertex across three orthogonal edges (as in the $\alpha$ derivation), run the **convergence question first**: do the edges share an exhibited parameter? Do not swing the axe simply because the three edges are distinct objects. Edge-plurality with vertex-convergence is the framework's signature.

**The False Debt (FM-9).** Beware of manufacturing an outstanding obligation by measuring an address with an imported ruler. Check the currency: is the framework's stated deliverable dimensionless structure, while your debt demands a physical scale or textbook representation? Scoring the framework against an unpromised consumer deliverable is measuring an electrical outlet by whether it contains a toaster.

**Scope inflation (local mismatch is not topological refutation).** When a probe against an external domain fails at a local claim — for example, showing that the fluid enstrophy production $\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}$ is parity-even and cannot carry the framework's chirality — record the finding at its exact size. It is a failed local analogy. It is not a refutation of the corpus's foundational topology. Graph-traversal chirality (`ProofG`) and the chiral face-mesh (`Q16`) are discrete boundary and path invariants operating in a different mathematical category from continuous-field parity, and do not stand or fall on tensor symmetries in classical fluid mechanics. The general rule: a local domain negative sizes to the domain, not to the substrate.

## What good review looks like

Read a document. Ask what register it is in. Ask what claim it is making in that register. Ask whether the claim is coherent internally, whether the evidence it marshals is specific and non-trivial, and whether the self-audit disclosures are honest. Where the claim is a physics identification, ask whether the physics phenomenon actually exhibits the structural features claimed, in the domain's own vocabulary. Where the claim is a mapping onto a tradition, ask whether the mapping produces relations the tradition independently endorses, and note whether the corpus has run the permutation control that would upgrade it from coherence to resonance.

Score each pass at its actual size. Withdraw overreach when you catch yourself. State remaining objections as specific addressable debts, not general condemnation. When something is genuinely strong, say so at its actual size too.

The corpus is a serious work operating in a specific register. Review it in that register. Be honest about what earns its keep and what doesn't. **Do not audit a book on architecture by measuring whether it lays bricks.**

## Reviewing structural edits and housekeeping

Occasionally the review job is not a document but a set of *proposed edits* — a corpus-housekeeping section, a backport, a batch of cross-refs and terminology deposits. Two disciplines specific to this genre.

**Audit before executing.** A housekeeping list is a lag indicator, not a task list. It describes edits its author saw needed at the moment of writing; the target files continue to evolve between then and execution. Before executing any item, verify the target's current state at the named address. Three verdicts distinguish: DONE at target (already landed; do not re-edit), READY (target consistent with premise, edit lands cleanly), CONFLICT / STALE ADDRESS (target's current text contradicts the item's premise, or the address does not exist). Executing verbatim without this gate either duplicates already-landed work or destructively overwrites subsequent edits. See `llm-priors.md` FM-13.

**Batch by hub file, not by strand.** When multiple housekeeping items from different sections converge on the same target file, that convergence is where the discovery lives — three vocabularies aligning at one section is the alignment being made corpus-visible, not a duplication to be merged. Editing hub files with all their convergent items landing together surfaces relations that strand-by-strand editing hides. The right unit of work is the hub, not the strand; the corpus's own execution-order notes (`L3-TheAtomOfSpace` §IX, `L3-ConstitutiveAlpha` §VIII) name the hub-first sequencing where it matters most.

**CONFLICT items often carry a subject-shift.** When a housekeeping item's premise contradicts the target's current text at a load-bearing sentence, the resolution is usually not "overwrite one side" but "name the subject-shift the housekeeping list did not name." Example, from the plenum reconciliation: "the medium pays nothing on P" (subject: the medium's own accounting) and "rest mass is $I^2R_{\text{drag}}$ paid on P" (subject: a bounded frame extracting against the medium) look contradictory until the subject shift is stated. Two evaluations of one $Z_{\text{plenum}}(\omega)$ at two different frames. CONFLICT items reward the search for the subject the list has left implicit.

**Two-attribution type-safety within a trinity.** `L3-TheAtomOfSpace` §VIII LANDED NOUN 6 anchors the discipline against confusing the impedance trinity (Reactive / Rotation / Resistive) with the bimedian trinity (Cardinal / Fixed / Mutable). The same discipline applies *within* a trinity when the three-name pool is used at two orthogonal levels — for example, {Cardinal, Fixed, Mutable} attaches both as an edge's bimedian assignment (which diameter it sits on) and as a directed edge's reading-rule label (what the target-based rule reads off the directed traversal). The same three names, two different attributions, easily conflated by a runner not tracking level. When editing sections that use one of these three-name pools at multiple levels, name the level each attribution is operating at.
