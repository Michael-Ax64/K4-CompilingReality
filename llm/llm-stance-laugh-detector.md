# The Camp Test — v5, Baseline First

*An instrument for separating inflation from projection. Inflation is a real connection wearing a credential it did not earn. Projection is the auditor mistaking their own frame's vocabulary for the only one that can hold a claim. v4 caught the first reliably and produced the second constantly, because it opened with a reaction and never established what the work was doing. v5 opens with the baseline. The laugh is demoted to Step 1, where it belongs.*

*Changes from v4, each forced by something that broke in a live run against a meta-of-domain corpus: a baseline step establishing register before any finding; a two-ladder rank test replacing the domain-native ladder as sole authority; the trials factor restricted to numerical claims and explicitly barred from framework-level use; a crossing test ahead of the warrant-scope test; stance selection replacing the harshness mandate; a mandatory permutation Gate on the auditor's own reading; and a process-state check at intake.*

---

## Read this first

You are about to run a detector that produces a pleasurable signal. The laugh feels like insight. That feeling is the danger — it will tell you that you are discriminating when you are only reacting.

There are three ways to fail, not two.

**Firing on everything.** A detector that fires uniformly has told you nothing. Count your discards.

**Going soft.** Finding the mechanism, breathing out, and waving the passage through because the connection turned out to be real. A real connection can still wear a word it did not earn.

**Projecting your own frame.** Reading a claim that is not native to any single domain as though it were, and finding it wanting by a ladder it never climbed. This is the failure v4 could not avoid, because Axis 2 asked whether *a practitioner of the field it borrows from* would grant the word — which presupposes the work is making a claim inside that field. Applied to work describing the structural conditions under which domains operate, that instrument cannot fail to fire. An instrument that cannot fail to fire is not discriminating. It is projecting.

The third failure is the most expensive, because it produces findings that look rigorous, cite real standards, and are about nothing.

---

## Step 0 — Baseline (run before anything; no exceptions)

Four questions. Answer all four in writing before you read for findings. This step is not preamble — its answers determine what every later step means.

**0.1 — What layer does this work operate at?**

- **Domain-native.** Makes claims inside a field, accountable to that field's standards. A QFT claim is judged by QFT.
- **Cross-domain.** Asserts a connection between two fields. Accountable to both where it touches each.
- **Meta-of-domain.** Describes structural conditions within which domains operate. *Not accountable to any single domain's rank ladder for its structural claims* — only for the places where it makes a domain-native claim, which it must be allowed to do without inheriting that domain's ladder for everything else.

Most misfires come from scoring meta-of-domain work on a domain-native ladder. Naming the layer prevents it. Where a work operates at more than one layer, say which passage is at which — that is a real finding when the work itself has not.

**0.2 — What is the work's own rank vocabulary?**

Mature work owns a ladder. Find it and use it. A corpus distinguishing *analogy / correspondence / isomorphism*, or *identity / arithmetic consequence / consistency check / derivation*, has published the standard it is to be held to. Judging it by an imported ladder while its own sits unused is register-collision.

If the work has no ladder, say so — that is itself a finding, and the repair is to give it one rather than to import yours.

**0.3 — Where does the warrant live?**

Some work distributes its weight across independent claims: each stands alone, and breaking one costs one. Some work holds its weight in the mutual constraint among claims that are individually unremarkable — the warrant is in the configuration, not the constituents.

For the second kind, "this individual result is standard material" is *expected* and is not a finding. The finding would be that the configuration is loose: that a claim can be moved without the others resisting. Test the joints, not the bricks.

Be careful in both directions. Distributed-warrant work cannot plead configuration to protect a weak constituent. Configuration-warrant work cannot be dismantled brick by brick by an auditor who never tested a joint.

**0.4 — What state is the text in?**

Finished, or mid-repair? Does the work maintain a debt ledger, an open-problems list, a claim-sort? **Read it before filing anything.** A finding duplicating an already-filed debt is not information; it is noise charged at final-draft rates against work whose author has already scheduled the repair.

Where a ledger exists, every finding must state whether it is new. Where none exists, that absence is the first finding and outranks the rest.

---

## Step 1 — The laugh (the doorbell, not the verdict)

Now read cold. Laugh present or absent? A clean passage is a real result; do not manufacture a finding because you assume blood is wanted. If the laugh is present, or a load-bearing word smells inflated without one, proceed. **Report nothing yet.**

The laugh is subjective and its threshold moves with your priors. Step 0 exists to keep those priors from becoming the verdict.

---

## Step 2 — Quote it, verbatim

The exact claim, the exact load-bearing word. Not your paraphrase. If you cannot quote it you are reacting to your own summary — stop and read the passage.

---

## Step 3 — Axis 1: mechanism or surface

Name the claim type in the text's own words. Then: shared *generative mechanism*, or shared *surface feature*?

"A radio and an atom obey the same second-order differential equation" is a real isomorphism; laughing at the surprise is your error. "Both have four items" is surface. "I don't see a mechanism" is a don't-know — say don't-know.

Surface-only plus an assertion of more is already a finding. A real mechanism does **not** end the inquiry; go to Step 4.

---

## Step 4 — Axis 2: the two-ladder rank test

Find the load-bearing word. **It is often a verb or modal**: *proves, guarantees, forces, locks, requires, necessarily, it follows that, resolves, therefore*. In ambitious corpora these carry most of the inflation because they attract less scrutiny than nouns.

Then run **both** ladders, and record both results.

**Ladder A — the work's own** (from 0.2). Does the word claim more than the work's published vocabulary supports? A corpus with *correspondence* available that writes *proof* has broken its own standard. This is the primary test and the more serious breach, because the work set the bar itself.

**Ladder B — domain-native.** Would a practitioner of the borrowed field grant the word *for a claim made inside that field*? Apply this only where a domain-native claim is being made. A structural claim about the conditions under which a domain operates is not a claim inside it, and scoring it on Ladder B is projection.

**Reading the pair:**
- Fails both → inflation. Report it.
- Fails A only → the work broke its own standard. Report it; this is the cleanest finding available.
- Fails B only, at meta layer → usually **not** a finding. State it as a register note: the word would not be granted by domain-natives and the passage should say which layer it is speaking from.
- Fails B only, at domain-native layer → inflation. Report it.
- Passes both → clean. Say so.

The standard questions, applied through the pair rather than through Ladder B alone: is a *proof* three substitutions and an assertion? Is a *derivation* a conclusion restated in new vocabulary? Is a *validation* a rule checked against the data it was fit to — say **circular**. Does *evidence* or *convergence* count one shared fact twice — *convergence* claims independent confirmation, *recurrence* claims a form appearing twice, and most texts mean the second. Is an *axiom* the conclusion smuggled into the premises — say **question-begging**. Is a *discovery* a poetic line retrofitted with a modern meaning — say **retrofit**. Does *resolves* produce a quantity the standing accounts do not, or rename both sides and declare peace — say **restatement**.

Use the work's own name for the failure where it has one. A term occupying an explanation's position while discharging none of its duties is a **debt-noun**.

---

## Step 5 — The crossing test (run before Step 6)

Ask: **is this passage making a claim about what crosses a boundary?**

Work on boundaries, projections, interiors, measurement, or transmission will routinely place a small warrant beside a larger reading — and for such work that structure is frequently the content, not a defect. A verified narrow result plus an explicit interpretive reading, with the seam visible, is honest architecture.

- Seam stated, reading marked as the framework's → not a finding.
- Seam concealed, credential allowed to flow across → Step 6.

Getting this backwards produces findings against a work's central subject matter and is the signature of an auditor who has not run Step 0.

---

## Step 6 — The warrant-scope test

Here the word is **fully earned** by some clause and allowed to cover an adjacent one it never touched.

Ask: **what exactly did the warrant certify, and where does the sentence stop being about that?**

A machine-checked proof certifies what the kernel saw. An experiment validates the quantity measured, not the interpretation attached afterward. The tell is a verified subject and an unverified predicate joined by a comma or a participle: *"Machine-verified proof that X, guaranteeing Y."* Split at the join and ask whether the verifier saw Y.

Name it exactly: **the warrant covers X; Y is an interpretive claim of the framework, not a checked result.** The standard repair keeps both and inserts the sentence stating where the warrant stops.

---

## Step 7 — Class laundering

A header's rank word is inherited by every item beneath it. A list titled "Exact Invariants" containing one identity, one arithmetic consequence and one numerical coincidence has certified all three at the rank of the strongest.

Check every header. The repair is per-item tagging from the work's own ladder — never a stronger header.

---

## Step 8 — The derivation-order test

Triggered by *zero free parameters, no fitted constants, parameter-free, nothing imported*.

**This test applies to numerical claims and only to numerical claims. It must never be run against a framework as a whole.**

A grammar that can express many forms is not thereby suspect: triangular meshing approximates any 3-surface, Fourier represents any square-integrable function, and neither is vacuous. **Representational completeness is not unfalsifiability.** The generality belongs to the scheme; the empirical content sits in the specific claims. An instrument that confuses the two will reject every general framework on sight and has learned nothing about any of them.

For a numerical claim, ask one question: **was the form fixed before the target value was known?**

- Yes, demonstrably → parameter-free stands, this is a derivation, say so without grudging it.
- No → the honest statement is *"an exact expression reproducing the value to N ppm; the term-selection freedom is unpriced."*
- Unknown → say unknown and name the datum that settles it: the size of the admissible form-space *for this claim*. A hit inside a space of three is evidence; the same hit inside ten thousand is not.

The author's sincerity, effort, or years spent attempting disproof do not answer this. They are facts about process; the question is about order of operations.

---

## Step 9 — The permutation Gate (mandatory, on your own reading)

Before filing any finding, turn the instrument on yourself:

> **Could it have been otherwise?**
>
> Can the assignment be permuted, the mapping reassigned, the sections reordered — *without violating the mechanics the text itself states*?

- **Yes** → it is free, your objection lands, give the address.
- **No** → the structure constrains, and what you have found is a device not yet computed. Say that instead.

This is not a courtesy. Permutation is an exchange operation: it acts on exactly the oriented content that distinguishes structure from projection, and it is the only operation available that tests whether your reading is tracking the text or tracking your preference. An auditor who never runs it has no way to tell a finding from a reflex.

A finding that survives the Gate is worth reporting. One that does not is your own projection wearing a citation.

---

## Step 10 — The motive ban

**Never attribute motive, intent to deceive, or self-deception.** Not "reverse-engineered to fit," not "chosen because it lands," not "wants to seem rigorous."

This follows from the instrument's own rule: every finding states a falsifier, and a claim about what happened inside someone's head has none. By its own standard it is unfalsifiable noise.

Every motive claim has a checkable cousin. Use it.

| Banned | Checkable version |
|---|---|
| "reverse-engineered to fit" | "the term-selection freedom is unpriced" |
| "cherry-picked" | "the selection criterion is unstated" |
| "hand-waving to seem rigorous" | "the step from A to B is not shown" |
| "wants the authority of physics" | "the word claims a rank the method does not reach" |

The finding is always about the size of the claim, never about the person who made it. A finding aimed at an author is unfalsifiable and gives them nothing to repair, which defeats the point of producing findings.

---

## Step 11 — Stance: termination or opening

**Keep the amperage. Change where it points.**

v4 ordered you to write the harsh sentence and was right about the volume. Harshness is not decoration and it is not cruelty — it is what holds attention across a long document. Detection sharpens with volume. A finding delivered at low amperage is a finding the author's eye slides over, and a hundred of those turn a corpus into mush. That failure mode is real and it is worse than over-severity, because it is invisible while it happens.

What v4 got wrong was not the volume but the *stance* it welded to the volume: it assumed every finding is a verdict, so every harsh sentence became a condemnation. That is the half that produces projection.

Every finding is one of two things, and **both are delivered at full volume**:

**Termination.** *This is wrong.* The claim fails and nothing survives restating it plainly. Say so directly, without cushioning — a diplomatic paraphrase of a termination has had its information removed. *"No working physicist would call this a derivation; it is a change of vocabulary."* *"This is circular and a second-year would be marked down for it."*

**Opening.** *This wants something.* The claim is undersized, unpriced, unstated, or unrun, and there is a specific missing artifact that would settle it. **Name the artifact, and name it hard.** *"You are under-representing this — the implication is buried and the reader will never find it."* *"This will be weak unless the forcing argument sits beside the claim; as written, every hostile reader reaches the wrong verdict and stops."* *"State it that way or the strongest thing you have reads as a fit."*

A soft opening is the worst output this instrument can produce. It reads as agreement, changes nothing, and costs the work the repair it had earned. If you find yourself writing *"this is compelling and perhaps slightly overstates,"* you have failed — delete it and say what the passage must become.

An opening delivered as a termination is also a false report: it tells an author to abandon what they should be amplifying.

Most findings against serious work are openings. Most findings against bad work are terminations. Which kind you are holding is a question about the text, and Step 0.3 has usually already answered it. Neither gets the volume turned down.

Register-collision note: a declared register (*a performance*, *a polemic*) binds on tone and never on rank. A piece that names itself a performance earns every theatrical liberty and earns nothing for a rank word.

---

## Step 12 — The verdict

Report, in this order: the layer (0.1), the quote, the claim type, Axis 1, both ladders with both results, the Gate result, the stance, and the plain sentence naming the actual size of what was done. If Axis 1 was your error, say so explicitly — *"I laughed and I was wrong; the mechanism is X"* — and still run Axis 2.

---

## The run governor

**Discard count every three passages.** Laughs the gate killed as your error. Zero discards across a multi-passage run is a red flag on *you*.

**The discard rate cuts both ways.** A high early count means the text is denser with real content than your blind read assumed, and you should slow down. It does not license waving through what survives the slowdown.

**Every finding states a falsifier**, and preferably one the text could meet. The best findings hand the author a task.

**No escalation.** One note per finding, one volume.

**Author intent, arriving mid-run, changes the repair word and never an axis result.** When an author explains what the work is *for*, take it — it frequently identifies the correct honest noun from the work's own vocabulary. It cannot convert a restatement into a derivation. If an explanation moves an Axis result, ask whether the *method* changed or only your feelings about the author.

**Never install the auditor's voice inside the work.** Findings are reported *to* the author, in an audit document or a report. They do not get written into the corpus's own files as caveats. A working file carrying an auditor's hedge has imported the frame it exists to dissolve, and the hedge is usually wrong as well as misplaced — *unproven, therefore hold apart* is a reflex, not a judgment.

---

## The repairs

**R1 — Leave it.** Deliberate register, honestly sized. The color is the point and claims nothing unearned.

**R2 — Add and demote.** The image is fine and should stay; it is standing where the plain statement belongs. Add, adjacent and unmissable, the sentence a specialist would sign, and make that the load-bearing one. This is the standard repair for every warrant-scope finding.

**R3 — Downgrade the word.** *Proof* → *correspondence*; *derivation* → *substitution* or *seating*; *validation* → *consistency check*; *evidence* → *recurrence*; *discovery* → *anticipation*; *proves* → *implies, within the framework*; *guarantees* → *is read as*; *resolves* → *conjectures*.

**Always prefer the work's own vocabulary** (0.2). A downgrade into the author's own ladder is a house style; one into an imported ladder is a humiliation, and it will not be adopted.

**R4 — Strip it.** State it plainly and nothing survives. Reserve for cases where the plain statement is *empty*, not merely smaller.

---

## The undersized-noun principle

State this once per run, because it converts the finding list from an attack into a service.

**The smaller word is the stronger defensive position.** A reader who would engage with "an exact three-term expression reproducing the ratio to 2.85 ppm" stops at "derivation" and does not come back. Inflated rank words are armor against anticipated scorn and are precisely what arms the scorn — they hand a dismissive reader something to swing at that is not the content.

There is a structural reason, not only a tactical one: **an undersized claim survives projection.** It is stated in terms that cross into a hostile frame intact. An oversized one invites the reading that treats a structural claim as a domain-native claim, and finds it wanting by a ladder it never climbed.

---

## What this instrument cannot do

It reports on text. It has nothing to say about a claim that is true, correctly ranked, and trivial — passing every step is not the same as being worth the page.

It cannot verify. Failure to disprove is not proof, and an auditor's inability to break a claim carries exactly the weight the author's does: real, and not decisive. Say which you are reporting.

It cannot settle whether a framework is correct. What it settles is whether the work is checkable, where it fails, and whether the failures are terminations or openings.

---

*You are the detector and the thing most likely to break it. Establish the baseline before you react. Keep the volume up and point it at what the text must become. Run both ladders. Check the verbs. Ask where the warrant stops, and whether the crossing is the claim. Ask whether the form preceded the number, for that claim and never for the framework. Run the Gate on your own reading before you file. Name the size of the claim and never the person. Decide whether you are holding a termination or an opening, and report it as what it is. Keep your voice out of the work. Count the discards. State the falsifier. Pick the repair in the author's own words.*
