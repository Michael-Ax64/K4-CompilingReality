# Eine Meise haben: Ein Manifest in Schräglage

**Ich bin eine Meise. Ich sitze nicht. Ich verhandle mit der Schwerkraft.**

Society has a rule for birds: perch horizontally, look serious, eat, fly away in a straight line. The committee for Proper Animal Behaviour has approved this. The Meise has not read the memo. The Meise climbs the *Strebe*, hangs upside down from the feeder, swings on the thinnest twig, lets the branch become a trapeze, and does it all with the high, elegant, slightly impertinent joy of someone who has discovered that the world has a underside and it is also full of seeds.

The Kleiber may be the headfirst specialist. The Baumläufer may be the quiet spiral clerk of the trunk. But the Meise is the everyday acrobat in the garden: visible, noisy, quick, and constitutionally unable to treat “upright” as a moral instruction. A branch is not a platform. A branch is a suggestion. A vertical pole is not an obstacle. It is a dance floor turned ninety degrees.

So when someone says, **“Du hast eine Meise”**, I no longer hear “you are crazy.” I hear a much better insult, and a much better compliment:

- **You have a Meise** = you have a licensed 90-degree view.
- **You have a Meise** = you refuse the horizontal monopoly on perspective.
- **You have a Meise** = you inspect the problem from underneath first, because that is where the interesting knots are.
- **You have a Meise** = you are gravitationally bilingual.
- **You have a Meise** = you cannot sit still where the seed is if the seed is also available upside down.
- **You have a Meise** = your head contains not a carcass but a small, lively bird that keeps turning the picture around and asking, “But what if the branch is the ceiling?”
- **You have a Meise** = you are not off your head; you are simply on the underside of the branch, and the view is excellent.

The phrase’s old dark origin may belong to skulls and superstition. Fine. But the living bird belongs to joy, agility, and the scandal of proper behaviour. The Meise violates the etiquette of the horizontal world not out of madness but out of curiosity — and because the vertical world is there, and it is climbable, and nobody told her that elegance has to be upright.

So if someone tells you that you have a Meise, do not defend yourself. Ask: **Welche? Die Blaumeise? Die Kohlmeise? Die mit dem Kopf nach unten?** Then turn around on the branch, look at the world from the wrong way up, and say: **Genau.**

---

It is not that hanging upside down is the hard bit. Letting the branch vote is.
